Investing in **Cryptocurrencies** is not as simple as one might think: choosing [the Exchange](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/quale-exchange/), finding something valid in a sea of junk, and also understanding whether to keep them in an Hot Wallet or a **Cold Wallet**.

This last point is of fundamental importance even if ignored by many.

It seems like a superfluous and useless thing, done only by those who are paranoid or who want to seem expert by using a **Wallet** external to the **Exchange**.

Well, that's not the case.

Keeping **Cryptocurrencies** in a studied place, whose security level is known, is not taken for granted. And it can save you from a great loss.

In fact, despite what one might think, **Cryptocurrency** thefts even on large **Exchanges** are not so rare.

And that is why we must understand well the options we have available.

The best one is **Cold Wallets**.

## The biggest Cryptocurrency thefts

**Exchanges** are not as safe a place as one might think.

The [**Wallet**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/dove-conservare-criptovalute/) where your Cryptocurrencies are held after purchase on the Exchange is an **Online Wallet**.

It is called **Hot Wallet** and obviously, to be able to exchange currencies, it must be connected to the network.

This connection is necessary but it is also dangerous.

If a connection exists, someone can cross it: with or without your permission.

**Hacker** attacks on **Cryptocurrency Exchanges** are not rare and for this reason, one must think about protecting oneself.

These are some of the most important attacks ever made.

- [**KuCoin**](https://www.kucoin.com/it) – September 2020

The Cybersecurity team of the KuCoin Exchange detects anomalous movements by some Wallets.

They don't even have time to block the attack that Hackers, in about two hours, stole **285 million dollars**.

- [**Coincheck**](https://coincheck.com) – January 2018

The Coincheck Exchange is one of the most important Japanese Exchanges.

Not much is known about this hit as the company affirmed the security and robustness of their security system. Despite this, **500 million dollars** were stolen.

- [**Ronin Network**](https://bridge.roninchain.com) – March 2022

Ronin Network is a Blockchain platform created for the play-to-earn game Axie Infinity.

In this case, the theft occurred due to an employee who fell for a fake job offer. The offer was made through a pdf file containing Spyware.

Through it and another error by the company, they managed to obtain the information needed to attack the platform.

The total theft was **540 million dollars**.

- [**Mt. Gox** ](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Gox)– 2013/2015

Mt. Gox at the time exchanged most of the Bitcoin in circulation. It wasn't an unknown Exchange, in short.

This robbery happened silently. The security of the platform, even if no one knew, had major flaws.

The robbery in this case was carried out silently. Over the years Hackers slowly withdrew Bitcoin, reaching the incredible figure of **800,000 Bitcoin**.

The assets were never returned.

At the time 800,000 Bitcoin were worth about **500 million dollars** but with the current exchange rate, they would be 14 and a half billion.

These are just some examples of thefts that have occurred in recent years.

Obviously, digital security is improving, and with the growth of **Cryptocurrencies** very large Exchanges are much more controlled.

But this does not bring the risk to zero, indeed it remains high.

## Do NOT keep them in an Exchange

I think you have already understood the risks of keeping all your **Cryptocurrencies** on an **Exchange**.

But why is it dangerous?

The wallet you use on an Exchange is an **Hot Wallet**.

Through it, it is possible to **exchange Cryptocurrencies** very quickly. And this is the greatest merit of an Hot Wallet.

But being connected directly to the network it is always at risk of theft.

It is a **wallet** that should be used to hold only a small part of all the capital you have invested. That small part that you might need to sell quickly.

## Cold Wallets: the best solution

**Cold Wallets** are the best solution.

They are **Offline**, so you cannot operate directly from them. It is as if they were a safe in which to keep one's savings.

In the **safe** assets are kept that will not be spent in the short term, and so a **Cold Wallet** works.

Most of your **Cryptocurrencies** must be held in a Cold Wallet.

Keeping them all in an **Hot Wallet** would be like keeping all your money in your **wallet**.

They are ready to be spent, this is convenient, but who would walk around with all their money in their wallet?

Besides the physical impossibility of keeping them all in a wallet, it would be **too high a risk**.

Same thing for Hot Wallets.

It is better to keep almost all of them in a savings account.

The **Cold wallet** will therefore serve to contain at least 90% of all your **Cryptocurrencies**. If your goal is **long-term investing** obviously.

It is certainly not a possible solution if your goal is to do [**trading**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/cosa-sono-i-futures-crypto/).

## Advantages of Cold Wallets

The main advantages of Cold Wallets are:

- **Tokens** will be 100% yours
- Maximum **Security**
- **Anonymity**

## Disadvantages of Cold Wallets

**Cold Wallets** obviously also have disadvantages, as one mentioned earlier.

In fact, it is not immediate to **spend** money that is on a cold wallet.

You need to transfer them to an Hot Wallet and then use them from there.

The other disadvantage is that all **responsibility** falls on you. You will have to be very careful not to lose your **tokens**.

## How Cold Wallets work

We have seen what they are, but **how do Cold Wallets work**?

### Types of Cold Wallets

**Cold Wallets** can be of two types: **Physical** (Hard Wallet) or **Digital**.

Digital Cold Wallets are usually **Software**. To access **Cryptocurrencies** you will always need the device on which the Software is installed, usually the computer.

**Hard Wallets** work the same way but **Cryptocurrencies** are held in a **physical** device, smaller than half a mobile phone.

It will therefore be necessary not to lose the device to access them.

### How They Work

How **Cold Wallets** work.

If you choose to use a **virtual wallet** you will probably have to download the software on your computer.

Instead, if you chose the **Hard Wallet** you will obviously have to buy it.

From this moment on, depending on the software or wallet used, the activation procedure will change slightly.

However, there are steps in common.

The main step, which characterizes **Cryptocurrency Wallets**, is the [**Seed Phrase**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/terminologia-crypto/).

The **Seed Phrase** is a phrase without complete meaning (a series of words) that act as a **password for the wallet**.

It is a type of password that is generated by the **software** you will use because it must be extremely **secure**.

This phrase allows accessing **Cryptocurrencies** even if the **Hardware** containing them is lost. However, you must always remember it and never lose it.

Anyone who manages to get your **seed phrase** will be able to access your **crypto**.

Usually, this phrase is written on a well-guarded piece of paper, or engraved on a small **metal plate**.

I repeat that it is of fundamental importance not to disclose it and not to lose it. The risk is not being able to ever access your **Crypto** again.

There are no strange password recoveries or anything like that. A disadvantage due to the fact that the **wallet** is offline.

When you have your **seed phrase** the wallet will be ready, you just have to send the **cryptocurrencies** to your new address.
