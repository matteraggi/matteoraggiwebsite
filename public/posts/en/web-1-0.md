## The origins of the Internet: what were the features of Web 1.0

Mass usage of the **Internet** arrived with [**web 2.0**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-2-0/), and now we are already talking about [**Web 3.0**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0/), but what could you do on the Internet when it was just born?

The answer to these questions is simple: almost nothing of what we do today on the Web.

The Internet was much simpler than it is now, and in fact, it is also called the **Static Web**.



The word "static" is used because in **Web 1.0** company-user interaction was zero.

What you could do was only **visit a website** belonging to a specific company and buy a product.

Using the word "only" may seem reductive considering the revolution that was taking place, but it is nothing compared to what we can do today.

There was no, as mentioned before, **interaction between companies and the consumer**. It was simply top-down communication: companies put products on the market and users could choose whether to buy them.

The fundamental block missing in this process was precisely listening to **consumer needs**.

## A "private" internet

A common person could not decide to create their own **space on the Web**; it was intended only for large multinational corporations.

There was no possibility of creating a **Blog** like this one, which is why it was a **Private Internet**, for the few.

Usage was mainly governmental: scientists and military for communicating, and as said before, also corporate.

It is with [**Web 2.0**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-2-0/) that the situation changes enormously. The first Blogs are born, so the presence of users becomes active, and **social networks** like Facebook and YouTube are born.

## Communication in Web 1.0: before Social Networks

Earlier I mentioned that in the **Static Web** / 1.0 communication was unilateral, so simply from company to user. But what does it mean?



Nowadays we have more methods of communication and the possibilities are endless. Both through social networks like Facebook and Instagram and through advertising like [Google Ads](https://ads.google.com/intl/it_it/home/).

With the use of these technologies, everyone can be on both sides: they can **both sell and consume**. And those who sell are in direct contact with consumers.

In **Web 1.0**, however, none of this existed and the user experience was viewing a page. Communication took place only via email, fax, or phone number.
