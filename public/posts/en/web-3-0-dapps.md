## What are DApps

DApps is the acronym for "**Decentralized Applications**".

Before going into detail, we need to understand two things:

* what is meant by **applications**
* what **decentralized** means

### What applications means

The applications we are talking about are not the typical smartphone applications, although obviously they can be, but they are much more.

They can be anything, such as a website or a service, but unlike what we have used and seen until today, they are decentralized.

So if previously applications concerned only the **smartphone** and were intended as a much simpler concept, in this case they can be real **Business models** based on decentralization (currently almost all are based on the **Ethereum** network).

### What Decentralized means

Centralized websites, applications, and services are created, managed, and controlled by a **company**. This means that if you want to use the service, they are the ones who decide the rules.

For example, if you are using the Instagram platform, their company team ([Meta](https://about.facebook.com/meta/)) can choose to **remove your content** if they don't want it on their service. The same goes for YouTube, Twitch, Google, and practically any platform we use on smartphones or computers.

Decentralized Applications serve precisely to avoid this. They have been programmed and created **not to be controlled** once launched on the market.

This means that there will be no one who can decide what people do on the platform and control how it is used. So it **will sustain itself**.
It remains a free product at the service of everyone, and this means Decentralized, that is, without a centralized "entity" that administers it.

Decentralization will be very important in [**Web 3.0**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0/), of which DApps are a part. And it is Web 3.0 that indeed places decentralization on the **Internet** as one of its strong points.

## How DApps work

Since DApps are built with **Blockchain**, their operation is regulated by it. Each user is a **node** of the network, which contains their own information and movements.

If you don't know what a Blockchain is, you can consult my article [Crypto Terminology](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/terminologia-crypto/).

Whenever a movement occurs in the network, it is **validated** on every node of the network.

This means that the application works thanks to every **single user**, who contributes to the operation. And the more users there are, the more **secure** the network is.

In addition to making the platform always **active** and **immune** to overloads like central servers, it makes it even more **decentralized**. Every user is worth the same and they all have the **same role**.

## Advantages and disadvantages of Decentralized applications

### Advantages

Some of the advantages can already be understood from the previous paragraph, such as greater freedom in using a service and the ability of the platform to "sustain" itself. However, let's see them one by one.

* **Security**

Decentralized applications are based on the blockchain. This means that the data of each person using a service is **accessible only to them**, and other people can only verify its validity.

This double operation allows both more **privacy** for users and more security for the platform.

As they say, it's a win-win situation.

Many believe that decentralized applications are less secure because they lack a team regulating their operation, but as just mentioned, this is not the case.

* **Decentralization**

Decentralization is a strong point because it allows the user base to act more **freely**.

Furthermore, decentralized applications are not subject to problems given by the need to control large amounts of data in central servers.

Decentralized platforms do not need them, so they cannot suffer a data overload, as has happened several times to Facebook, Instagram, and Whatsapp.

They don't need them because the Blockchain records all data and transactions of a user on their own "profile". Basically **everyone owns their own data** and they are not contained in a single centralized place.

* **Open source**

This is not a new feature, but it is still a common factor of DApps.

In addition to making the application much more **transparent**, contrary to what one might think, it makes it more secure.

Yes, I know, it seems strange. With the source code available to everyone, it will be even easier for Hackers to find flaws in the system. And this may be true. But you must keep in mind that it is also easier for "good" users to manage to **find problems** and fix them.

Considering that "bad" users are much fewer, it is more difficult for them to find **flaws** in a decentralized platform.

For example, the **Linux** operating system, which has existed for quite some time and is open source, is considered very secure precisely for these reasons.

### Disadvantages

One of the main disadvantages is actually due to the use of the Blockchain. It is thanks to it that all the merits listed before exist, but it also brings a **negative aspect**.

This is the forced correlation between Blockchain and application. What does it mean?

It means that if you develop an application on Ethereum your progress is restricted to what the **ETH network** can do. Basically, if you want to improve your project you will have to **submit** to the capabilities of the Blockchain you use, and therefore you will have to **wait** for it to make improvements as well.

A further obstacle can be the difficulty of developing some DApps, so complicated as to make the **implementation** of some features truly difficult.

Finally, many DApps, for the moment, have a very poor **user interface** and this discourages users from using them.

Certainly, it is also because it is still early and many have been born recently. In a few years, it is hoped that the situation will be better.

## The main differences with centralized applications

What are the main **differences** between Decentralized Applications and traditional ones?

All types of applications are composed of three parts: Frontend, Backend, and Data Storage. This is a simplified structure but enough to understand the **differences**.

First, let's understand what these **levels** are.

### Frontend

The Frontend is the level **visible** to users, that is, the **interface** that is displayed by them.

It only serves to allow the user to interact at best, but it does not determine the operation.

Both DApps and classic applications possess **advanced graphic resources**, through the use of [HTML](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML) and [CSS](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSS).

### Backend

Also in this case, from the name, you can understand what the role of the level is.

It is indeed "**behind**" and is not visible to the user, but only to the **programmer**.

This part of the application serves to build its operation, and it is here that most of the differences lie.

DApps work, as mentioned earlier, through the Blockchain. The **Backend** will therefore include a **Smart Contract** structure executed on Ethereum or Solana, for example.

However, in the case of DApps, implementation with the Blockchain allows making part of the Backend **visible** to users.

Smart contracts are visible to everyone, while in the case of a traditional application, the entire Backend is **covered**.

### Data Storage

The difference in the **data storage** level is very important in this comparison because it is one of the major **advantages** of Blockchain implementation.

In centralized applications, user data is contained in **large databases** in central servers, which due to this large load of data can also manifest some problems.

With the Blockchain we do not have this problem, because data is also **decentralized**. It is recorded in the Blockchain and obviously obscured from unauthorized persons.

Thanks to the **saving** of the entire movement history directly in the Blockchain, just download/use the DApps on another device to have all your information **automatically**.

## Examples of DApps

### [Cryptokitties](https://www.cryptokitties.co)

This is one of the first games made on Blockchain. It is certainly not the most useful application but it is nice to know that **video games** are also being developed on the Ethereum network.

### [ChainLink](https://chain.link)

This is a DApp with greater utility. Chainlink wants to promote an open standard for decentralized data input that validates information from external feeds. Basically, it serves to **protect data** to and from a smart contract.

Even **Google** itself is testing it as an intermediary for data on its platform.

### [Decentraland](https://decentraland.org)

**Decentraland** is a decentralized virtual world, a Metaverse, in which you can build a **parallel life** to that in the real world.

I talked about it in more depth in another article of mine: ["Decentraland what is it and how does it work"](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/decentraland/).

You can buy land, build houses, visit other people and attractions, buy clothes, and much more.
