**Tilemap or Freehand Drawing?**

An important decision to make in the early stages of development concerns the use of Tilemaps for game map generation.

The Tilemap is the most commonly used method and consists of creating connectable pixel art blocks to then use them to draw all parts of the map.

With freehand drawing, on the other hand, one could achieve infinitely greater customization, with more random and natural lines, making the game aesthetically less schematic and rigid.

Obviously, this solution would require much more time, especially for maps of a certain size.

As for the Tilemap, implementation would certainly be simpler, but it would be complex to create a design that isn't banal and blocky. Wanting to create a design that transports the player into the game world, it is important not to create a detached view and a blatantly fake world.

It is also true that we are only talking about the background, which can then be embellished with upper decoration layers.

Unfortunately, even though I believe the game would look better with the first option, I will opt for the second, given that the first is practically impossible to carry forward alone. And it is also more complex to make good pixel art without following the Tilemap patterns.

After making this decision, I created and inserted the prototype asset of the protagonist, without animations, but capable of moving in the game map that currently exists.

**Pixel Art Perspective**

Perspective is important, and it is what makes the difference in a pixel art game, especially top-down.

If you want to emulate the perspective of images generated by ChatGPT or similar, I believe they are at 45° or lower, because in some scenes they look almost 90° like a horizontal platformer. Here it would be a middle ground.

The 3/4 perspective consists of having 1 of x, 3/4 of h, and 3/4 of z. Basically, it is a bit squashed in height.

![45 degrees perspective photo](/foto_giocoinsetti/45gradi.png)

If desired, one can also lower the angle a bit, thus decreasing the view of the upper part of the elements.

![20 degrees perspective photo](/foto_giocoinsetti/20gradi.png)

**Isometric vs. Oblique Projection**

The photos and information for the perspective were taken from [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ycke8oznJbk).

Isometric perspective is the one used as above; objects take on a different perspective if seen from above. For example, circles tend to become ovals.

In oblique perspective, however, the plane is not tilted but straight, and therefore objects at the top maintain their normal size.

Often small changes, even if technically wrong, are better for design.
