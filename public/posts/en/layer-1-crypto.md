Yes, as you may have understood by now, the structure of a **Blockchain network** is not as simple as it might seem.

Similarly to [**Internet**](https://www.tecnotrade.it/news/networking-modello-iso-osi/), it is divided into levels/**layers**, each of which plays a very precise role.

In this article, you will discover what **Layer 1** is in **Crypto** and what it deals with.

To understand what **Layer 1** is, it is better to first understand the structure of the **Blockchain** as a whole: what levels exist and what they are used for.

## The Layer structure of the Blockchain

What are these levels?

- **Layer 0**

Often included in level 1, when present it plays the role of both **physical** and **digital** infrastructure of the network, dealing for example with **security**.

- **Layer 1**

What we are talking about in this article, without it the network could not exist. It is the heart of the **Blockchain**.

- **Layer 2**

It includes all **protocols** based on the network. They perform most functions and express the full potential of the Level 1 Blockchain.

These protocols cannot be integrated directly into the first **Layer** due to a [**Scalability**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/trilemma-blockchain/) problem.

- **Layer 3**

It includes all [**Web 3.0 applications**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web3/) that rely on the networks of the previous levels (1 and 2). So for example **Decentralized Exchanges** based on **Ethereum** (Layer 1) or **Polygon** (Layer 2).

- **Layer 4**

It represents the interfaces of the previous level. So the [**DApps**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0-dapps/), **NFT** Marketplaces, or wallets like [**Metamask**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/metamask/).

## Layer 1 crypto: what is it and what does it deal with

But what does **Layer 1** specifically deal with?

If all major applications and functions are in the **subsequent Layers**, what is Layer 1 for?

Why is it so important?

Let's try to answer all these questions.

As we have already seen, Layer 1 is the most important of all. The **Bitcoin** network itself was born without **Layer 0**, so its functions are included in this first level.

These cases actually represent most **Blockchains**.

In these cases, Layer 1 performs the following **functions**:

1. **Cryptography** and data **security**
2. How all **Layers** communicate with each other
3. Management of the [**consensus algorithm**](https://checkpointbitcoin.it/algoritmo-di-consenso/), confirmation of blocks, and execution of **transactions**

As you may have understood, level 1 **Blockchains** are the most famous, for example: **Bitcoin**, **Ethereum**, **Solana**, and **Cardano**.

To make you better understand the difference between the other levels, I'll give you further examples.

Layer 0: **Cosmos**, **Polkadot**. They only give the structure to then develop Layer 1s.

Layer 2: **Polygon**, **Loopring**, **Lightning Network**.

If instead a Layer 0 exists, Layer 1 performs only the functions of consensus and execution. The rest is left to the previous level.

## The Scalability Problem and Solutions

Why isn't everything implemented directly on **Layer 1**?

- **Scalability** problem

The Scalability problem is a recurring problem in the **Blockchain** world. Ever since **Vitalik Buterin**, one of the creators of Ethereum, made the world aware of the [**Blockchain Trilemma**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/trilemma-blockchain/).

A term coined by him, it indicates the problem that every Blockchain network must face.

It is about finding a **balance** between these properties that a network should guarantee:

- **Security**
- **Decentralization**
- **Scalability**

Increasing one decreases another.

In particular, the problem concerns guaranteeing good **scalability** to the network without losing the other two properties.

The **Ethereum network**, as well as all others, had to face problems given by a large influx of users.

This large number of visits congests the network, increasing **transaction costs** and slowing it down.

A **scaling** solution must be found that does not greatly reduce the other two parameters. And every **Blockchain developer** is looking for solutions that can reach a better compromise.

Yes, we talk about compromises because, at least for our current knowledge, the problem is 100% **unsolvable**.

This is the same reason that leads to the creation of numerous **solutions**, without a real metric that can classify them.

In any case, we can distinguish all solutions into two sections:

- **On-chain** Scaling
- **Off-chain** Scaling

The former are direct modifications on **Layer 1**. They are usually longer and more complicated.

The latter deal precisely with the Layer structure. **Off-chain Scaling** is precisely the creation of **secondary Layers** to lighten level 1.

The purpose of **Layer 2** (comment if you want an article about it) is therefore not to affect Layer 1, so not to modify the main **Blockchain**.

It is rather to help the **network** by acting outside the **main chain**.

What are these solutions? Let's see the best ones, both on-chain and off-chain.

### Layer 1: Increasing block size

The first method, but probably the least solving, is the increase in the **block size** of the network.

Clearly, increasing the size will allow each block to contain more **transactions**.

But there are problems:

- **temporary** solution
- increases the **Blockchain size**
- decreases **decentralization**

An example of the application of this method is [**Bitcoin Cash**.](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/it-it/impara-come-operare-con-criptovalute/cosa-e-bitcoin-cash)

### Layer 1: Change of consensus mechanism

Changing the **consensus mechanism** is the best-known method.

You know **Ethereum**? I guess so.

Previously the Ethereum network had a **Proof of Work** consensus mechanism (like Bitcoin's), then it decided to move to a **Proof of Stake** mechanism.

The difference between the two methods?

- PoW is more **decentralized** (easier to enter the network)
- PoS is **faster** and has lower commission costs

A recurring factor you will often find is that with the reduction in the number of **processors** (therefore the decrease in the network and decentralization) **speed** increases.

### Layer 1: Sharding

**Sharding** is a process of partitioning the **Database** into small fragments (shards).

They are faster and easier to control. This is a very used technique because it manages to reduce **transaction costs** and increase scalability.

It is also very simple to implement, just partition the database.

However, problems could arise in case an entire **shard** fills up.

In that case, it will be necessary to move a user and all their data to another **fragment**, a non-optimal operation.

## Off-chain solutions

Most scalability solutions are **off-chain**. **Layer 2** relies on and uses the infrastructure of the main **Blockchain**.

It adds new features and lightens the tasks of the **network**, helping it not to congest.

We could say that **Layer 1** deals with the fundamentals of the Blockchain, therefore decentralization and security, while **Layer 2** deals with **performance** and features.

The main off-chain solutions are:

- **Nested Blockchain**
- **State Channel**
- **Sidechain**
- **Relay Chain**

We have reached the conclusion of this article. I hope I have clarified every doubt of yours. Before going here is a small summary...

**Layer 1** is the most important level for a **Blockchain** network, and it is the only mandatory one.

Without it, no Blockchain exists. It deals together with **Layer 0** (if it exists) with the most important tasks of a Blockchain: **Cryptography**, Layer communication, management of the **consensus algorithm**, and execution of **transactions**.

This does not mean that subsequent Layers are not important. Without them, the network would be poorly **scalable** or devoid of functions.

## FAQ

**What are Layer 1 crypto?**

Layer 1 is the most important level of the Blockchain. Layer 1 Crypto are the native cryptocurrencies of the platform. While Layer 2 crypto rely on secondary protocols.

**What are Layer 1 and 2?**

Layer 1 is the main level of a Blockchain network, without it the network does not exist.
Layer 2 is a secondary level, based on the first. All protocols based on the previous one can be born on it.
