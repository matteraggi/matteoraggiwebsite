**Reading** is fundamental when approaching a new world and so much new **information** to learn.

There are books that contain information you already know, but somehow organize it and create greater clarity, while there are books that teach you a lot: new perspectives or new information.

In this mini-guide, I try to collect the books that I liked the most regarding **Personal Finance**.

## Personal Finance Books: The Classics to Start With

### Rich Dad Poor Dad, Robert T. Kiyosaki

Rich Dad Poor Dad is probably **the most famous book** regarding Personal Finance, and it is the one that everyone recommends to anyone who wants to read about this topic.

It is a book that amazes for its simplicity, for the teachings, but also for the ability to entertain. Reading it manages to change the way one **thinks about money** and the way one **manages** it.

**Robert Kiyosaki** focuses on a main concept: do not spend money on assets that lose value, but choose those that appreciate.

### The Richest Man in Babylon, George S. Clason

The Richest Man in Babylon is as if it were **the Gospel of personal finance**.

It tells what the fundamental points are for managing one's money and reaching the path of **wealth**.

It is a very clear book, which everyone should have, very small and compact, quick to read.

What it wants to express remains impressed on the reader despite the length.

The teachings are accompanied by a magical setting, as the title says: in **ancient Babylon**.

### Think and Grow Rich, Napoleon Hill

This Book, in addition to talking about financial growth, speaks precisely of **personal growth**, of self-realization.

A book that helps transform one's dream into reality, giving you important incipits that, if applied, can change your life.

### Secrets of the Millionaire Mind, T. Harv Eker

How do you spend your money? How much of it is put aside? How much is **invested**?

The answer to these questions can be indicative of your **financial future**.

The author of this book tries to help not to commit these errors, to allow you to accumulate wealth.

This too, like the previous ones, is fundamental to start understanding a **new mindset**.

### The 4-Hour Workweek, Timothy Ferriss

The 4-Hour Workweek is a book that talks about **financial freedom**, but unlike others, it uses a different key.

It tries to focus on the **life change** you could obtain, and on the true meaning of wealth.

**Wealth** is not in fact given by your salary, but is correlated to your **time**.

If you earn €10k every month but work 12h a day you will not be richer than a person who earns half but works 4h.

This concept is important to understand what wealth actually is.

It is a book that spurs you on and helps you change your life, highly recommended.

## Personal finance books: for those who have already read something

### Money: Master the Game, Anthony Robbins

This book, unlike the previous ones, penetrates more deeply into personal finance.

I do not consider it an introductory book to the topic but an in-depth one, although it is not a complicated read.

Like The Richest Man in Babylon, this book is also structured in points: in this case, there are **7 simple steps**.

These steps have the task of guiding you towards what will be a more **financially secure** future. Also very interesting is the section dedicated to interviews with prominent people in the financial world like **Ray Dalio**.

### The Total Money Makeover, Dave Ramsey

The author, thanks to his experience gained over the years, has managed to create a practical program to achieve precisely financial freedom.

The first step, however, happens in the mind, inside us, in our **way of thinking** and in our **lifestyle**.

Only after these changes will we manage to achieve it.

### The Psychology of Money, Morgan Housel

The Psychology of Money is another very interesting book that provides interesting concepts without being heavy.

They are all expressed simply and through brief explanations, for each of the points covered.

While having traits in common with other books, it talks about new themes such as luck, the **utility of optimism**, and the crazy things rich people do.

## The 5 AM Club

The **5 AM Club** is a book completely **different** from what you expect, and is slightly different from the previous ones.

It does not speak only of personal finance, nor only of getting up at 5 every morning, but speaks of **personal improvement** in every field.

Both **financial** and **personal**.

Furthermore, it is a particular book, which tells a **very strange story** to better immerse you in the writer's philosophy.
