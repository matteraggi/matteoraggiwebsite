## Not just Cryptocurrencies

Cryptocurrencies are not a simple market to operate in.

The more volatile a market is, the more opportunities there are. However, opportunities must be seized.

And it is difficult to operate in a less liquid market, therefore more subject to sudden price changes. And unexpected ones.

For this reason, one of the most important tips I feel like giving you is precisely to avoid operating only on the Crypto market.

I understand the temptation can be high. Especially because:

- It is easier to earn (but also lose money)
- If you are on this article you are interested in Crypto

But remember that it is your money. And Trading, especially as an inexperienced person, can be really dangerous.

Psychological management is what makes the difference, and it starts right here: the decision of the market in which to operate.

I'm not telling you not to operate on Crypto. Also because if your final goal is that, it is also right to start getting familiar with it immediately. But not only that.

I advise you to flank it with the classic Forex market or Gold.

And remember that the best Traders do not earn exorbitant amounts from their winning trades. Rather, they focus on excellent Stop Loss management, in order to minimize losses.

Fewer losses, less chance of being in the negative. Which is the thing to avoid most of all.

## Start with little

We tend to want to start immediately like a professional.

8 curved screens, 200 indicators open, and 10 charts open simultaneously.

There is no need for all this. At the beginning, it is better to keep your feet on the ground.

Starting with many indicators all at once runs the risk of not even understanding them. And not really learning when they should be used and when not.

Or a classic is copying a set of indicators seen online by some creator. This is an option I do not recommend you pursue because it risks not making you understand even what you are doing.

This way you will never learn.

So what do I recommend doing?

I recommend starting with only one indicator at a time and trying to understand:

- how it is calculated
- what it is for
- when it should be used
- if it is useful in the crypto market
- if it can be replaced with something more accurate

And slowly skim off all superfluous indicators.

We often tend to think the opposite but less is always more.

An accurate selection of indicators that we know perfectly and that we know for certain work is certainly better than dozens of random indicators.

If you do the study and testing work accurately you will be able to trust yourself and use only a chosen Set of indicators.

Think about this:

If there were an indicator that, 100%, managed to tell us with certainty the price trend, just that one would be enough for perfect performance.

Obviously, this does not exist, but the best thing to do is to find a set of tools that allows us to get as close as possible to the result. And the set of tools must be minimal.

## Take inspiration, don't copy

I already talked about it above. And it seems like a trivial thing, but most beginner Traders fall into this trap.

But it's not just about watching a video and imitating the behavior seen shortly before. It can be much worse.

Does "trading signals" ring a bell?

I get shivers just writing it. I'll explain briefly for those who have never heard of it.

They are groups (usually telegram or discord channels) where trading operations are sent.

The only thing a member of this group has to do is copy the operation. Oh and obviously access to the channel costs tens of Euros every month.

The price is clearly justified by the fact that THEORETICALLY you should recover the investment in a short time.

The problems with this type of service:

- You don't learn sh*t
- Often the much-advertised returns are false

Apart from the first statement, which seems clear enough to me (how can you learn by just copying? Without thinking?), I'll explain the second one.

Without naming names, I'll give an example of a Trading room that advertised triple-digit percentage returns every month. The problem?

That the monthly return was calculated by summing the percentage returns of individual operations, but it's not like that for two reasons:

1)

Often operations are structured with multiple profit points. First Step: 50% of the position sold. Second Step: 50% of the remaining position. And final step with the rest.

In this case, you cannot calculate the percentages of the three profit points equally, finding ourselves at the last one with only 1/4 of the invested capital.

2)

In this trading room I'm talking about, they summed the percentages instead of calculating them consecutively.

What do I mean?

Let's pretend that the first 3 operations of the month go into a loss and your capital drops to €70 from the initial €100.

The fourth and last operation of the month goes very well, however, with a 100% profit, bringing the €70 to a final €140.

Percentage calculated by them:

-10% (first op) -10% (second op) -10% (third op) + 100% (fourth op) = 70% profit

But it's not like that. The 70% profit on the initial capital of €100 would lead us to have €170. Instead, we have €30 less.

(this is an example with random percentages and profits)

The price difference is given by the fact that the positive percentage is actually to be calculated on the capital reduced by previous operations.

Summary: little knowledge of mathematics or attempt to falsify profits (for profit, therefore reportable).

## Watch out for news

An important parameter to keep in mind is market news.

And no, I'm not advising you to go Long on DogeCoin as soon as Elon Musk posts on Twitter.

Quite the opposite.

The first times you approach the market it is important to understand what you are doing right and what not.

And it is also important to be very precise about it, so as not to get confused more than necessary.

News can disturb you in this process.

You might misjudge an indicator or a technique you are using just because the price behaves in a completely opposite way to what you believed.

However, the movement may be caused by sudden news and not by your error.

Especially at the beginning, one does not pay much attention to when market news should come out, not being the most important thing.

So you might not be informed. What I advise you is to be careful to evaluate the price movement well, also evaluating these rarer events.

## No Demo accounts

I know, almost everyone recommends starting with Demo accounts.

(Demo accounts are accounts with fake money, used to test techniques and practice)

But I don't recommend them.

Do you know what is the fundamental ingredient in Trading? The hardest one to learn? The one that will make you lose the most money and that few have?

It is not a technical skill, quite the opposite. It is the ability to manage anxiety. Anxiety about losing money.

This psychological factor is fundamental. And no, it is absolutely not to be underestimated.

I'm not joking, you can be as good as you want, but if you can't control yourself from this point of view any strategy could be useless.

And this is the big problem with demo accounts. This component is missing.

Managing fake money is a completely different thing. There is no thrill, there is no anxiety. Everything is much easier.

What I advise you is not to use them, but to start immediately with a real account. Even if with small amounts.

This can already make a big difference.
