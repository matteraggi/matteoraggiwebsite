### The principles of Bitcoin – The Whitepaper

I studied the White Paper for you (you're welcome). And now I'll explain the main points.

- **Introduction**: Why was it created?

It wants to replace the entire banking system.

The **banking system** is a trust-based system, and this is because there are human intermediaries (for every operation).

And this is not exactly the definition of security, especially when it comes to money.

Furthermore, transaction times and especially **costs** increase.

Bitcoin wants to find a way (in fact, it has found a way) to allow a **digital exchange** without intermediaries. Safely.

In fact, it will not be necessary to trust anyone, neither who sends us the money nor any third person (because there aren't any).

Everything is managed by the **network**. Automatically.

- **Transactions**

I won't tire you with technicalities. Basically, Bitcoin works through:

- a **public ledger**
- users (computers/processors) who verify the transaction

Instead of the classic banking system with a private ledger and a transaction verification system controlled by **central banks**.

Everyone can be a user of the network and for this reason, it is very decentralized.

If the majority of people say "Yes, the transaction is valid" then it will be accepted by the network. Otherwise vice versa.

So to **attack the network** a malicious person would have to possess more than half of all "nodes" (users).

Besides being almost impossible it is also inconvenient. You'll understand why shortly.

- **Proof of Work** – Mining

We were talking about users/computers contributing to the network, confirming transactions or not.

The work they do is not charity, but has a name: **Mining**.

Miners (those who do Mining) are rewarded in Bitcoin.

This is necessary because the work a computer on the network has to do is quite complex. And therefore consumes energy.

Are you thinking of starting and Mining to **earn Bitcoin**? I dedicated a paragraph to it, be patient.

The rewards given to Miners, however, have other functions:

- produce new currency
- prevent hacker attacks

Point 1: Bitcoins given to Miners are **new Bitcoins**, issued by the network. They are fewer and fewer as time passes, to make it a deflationary asset.

Point 2: if a malicious person were to take over more than half of the network (practically impossible) they could control all transactions.

But in this case, thanks to the rewards, it would be convenient for them to use their obtained power for good.

They would earn more from Mining rewards than from illicit acts. That is why it is inconvenient.

- **Privacy**

*"We were talking about a public ledger and a decentralized network open to everyone. How does it guarantee privacy?"*

Good question. The answer is actually very simple, just look at the **public ledger** of Bitcoin.

As you can see, the ledger is indeed public but there is no name present.

In fact, the ledger uses as an identifier the **public key** of the wallet that performed the operation. Can the owner's name be traced from the public key?

Yes and no. State authorities might have the power to do so, following movements until reaching a centralized platform.

But surely no ordinary person will be able to recognize you.

For example: Global Equity Funds or Bonds.

## And what if I start mining Bitcoin instead?

The situation is quite complicated.

And it is because starting to **mine** involves considerable expenses.

The expenses are:

- purchase, renewal, and maintenance of equipment
- electricity

So we need to calculate what our monthly expenses and our income would be.

Most Bitcoin "**farms**" are underground or in places with very low average temperatures. Or even where electricity is cheap.

Low temperature allows machines to cool naturally, always saving on the main cost: **electricity**.

In fact, if you can't save much on equipment because prices don't vary much in the end, electricity costs can be greatly reduced.

You should calculate based on your electricity plan how much you would pay. But I warn you, it probably isn't convenient in Italy.
