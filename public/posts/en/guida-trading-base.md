## Introduction to Technical Analysis

**Technical Analysis** is based on three main premises:

- The market discounts everything
- Prices move in trends
- History repeats itself

The first statement summarizes the fact that **analysts** are not interested in external events that can influence the price, since everything is already inside the chart. The **chart** itself already contains everything we need.

The second statement is fundamental and all the **analysis** we will see takes it for granted. It is always more likely that a **trend** continues its course rather than reverses it, so until there are reversal signals it is destined to continue.

"History repeats itself" is a statement concerning human psychology, very connected to **price analysis**. Man and his way of reasoning tend not to change, so by studying past prices we can intuit future movements.

Technical Analysis can be applied to different types of **time frames**, in fact, there are different **Trading strategies**:

- **Scalping**, with operations in the order of minutes
- **IntraDay**, closing every operation within the day
- **Day Trading** (Swing), with a 24h time frame
- **Position**, open long-term positions (unlike long-term investors, **timing** counts)

## How a chart is made

There are different types of charts but the most used is the **candlestick**, so the candlestick chart.

From the chart we can derive: maximum, minimum, opening, and closing price.

The bars at the bottom of the chart represent the **volume** for each candle.

## The Fundamentals

The market can go up or down and has two main **Trends**: **Bullish** and **Bearish**. The phase in which the market moves sideways is an intermediate phase still internal to the **main trend** in progress.

Then we can divide trends by importance: we have the **primary trend** (lasting even a few years), the **secondary trend** (corrections of the primary trend), and the **minor trend** (market fluctuations).

The Main Trend can also be divided into phases of: **accumulation**, **public participation**, and **distribution**.

The accumulation phase is the phase after a price drop, when informed **investors** enter the market, the public participation phase is when the price rises following the arrival of the **masses** and the distribution phase is the one in which the first investors begin to **exit the market**, distributing the securities to newcomers.

### To keep in mind

- **Indices** must agree with each other, analyzing a single index is not enough to act on the chart. It is the study from multiple points of view that, when we obtain the same result from every perspective, must lead us to **operate**.
- **Volume** is important and serves to **confirm** that a trend is valid. When we notice a decisive price trend, but lacking volume, something is wrong.
- A trend continues until there are clear **reversal** signals. But it is not easy to recognize them.

## Market Trends

All technical analysis, as I mentioned before, is based on monitoring and analyzing **Market Trends**.

Obviously, a trend will not be a straight line but, as you saw before too, the market is characterized by **continuous oscillations**. The Trend is identified by highs and lows. Highs and lows always higher, bullish trend. Highs and lows always lower, bearish trend.

So, how can we study the **progress of a Trend**?

We use the first tools at our disposal: **supports** and **resistances**, which are based only on price.

### Supports, resistances, and Trendlines

I explained to you how the progress of **market trends** is seen from the analysis of highs and lows, which we use to trace supports and resistances.

Lows are the points where the price **bounces**, and are called **supports**. It is therefore a price zone that **supports the trend**. Highs are the points where the price "**hits**", and are called **resistances**. It is a price zone that cannot be overcome.

These price levels are useful for us to understand when the trend can continue and when there is a risk of reversal. In fact, the loss of one of these levels can be a **trend reversal** signal.

But as I told you before Trends tend to continue, so in conjunction with these critical levels one "**bets**" that the price bounces or hits and the trend continues.

When areas of interest are lost, previously opened **positions** are closed.

Furthermore, when one of these levels is overcome the roles are swapped: the previous resistance will act as support and vice versa.

Usually, supports and resistances are intended as **horizontal lines**, but they can also be oblique and in this case, they take the name of **Trendlines**. They are practically the union of multiple support or resistance levels.

It may happen to identify two **parallel trendlines**: one of support and one of resistance. In that case, we would have found a descending or ascending **channel** where the price oscillates.
It can be excellent for opening and closing new positions on the touches of the lines.

This is a perfect example.
The **trendline** that acted as resistance to the price, once overcome, acted as support.

As you see tracing these lines helps us better understand the market trend.

## Trend Reversal Formations

The main difficulty in recognizing these **chart structures** is distinguishing them from **continuation** structures.
To which they are very similar.

The difference is that a reversal phase prepares us for a change of trend, while the continuation one is just a pause, after which the current trend continues.

The utility of these structures is precisely the possibility of recognizing when they are still in progress, so as to profit from them.

The **most common figures** are:

- Head and Shoulders
- Double or Triple top / bottom
- Rounding bottom and Spikes

**Volume** can help us a lot. Both in distinguishing from other figures and in analyzing the validity of the movement.

First of all, let's list a series of common factors to keep in mind when talking about this type of formation:

- The first trend reversal signal is always the **break of a Trendline**
- The wider and longer-lasting the formation, the more powerful the subsequent **movement** will be
- Usually **Top** formations (reversal during an Uptrend) are much faster than **Bottom** formations (reversal during a Downtrend).

### "Head and Shoulders" Formation

I start immediately by showing you what it is about. Seeing it graphically first will help you better understand what we are talking about.

It is the most important formation, also because most **reversal** figures are variations of this one. It also exists in reverse for **Bottom** formations, in that case, it is called "**Inverse Head and Shoulders**".

Think for example of a Double or Triple Top/Bottom. The Double is the same but lacks the head, the Triple has the head as high as the shoulders.

How do we distinguish it from a **continuation** structure?

After the creation of Shoulder 1, the price rises up to the Head (we won't know yet that they are).

We check volumes: if they are related to the **price increase** (increase with the creation of the head) then we have no alarm bell for the moment. If they do not exceed the volume values of the shoulder creation, we have a problem.

It could be a first signal of **loss of strength** of the Bullish Trend, but we still do not have decisive signals.

The situation worsens when the price corrects below Shoulder 1, piercing it.

Since it was a previous **maximum**, it acted as support, but it was not enough to make the price **bounce**.

At this moment we have the certainty that the **Bullish Trend** has stopped, but it could be just a **slowdown**, a momentary drop.

With the creation of Shoulder 2 (Lower High) the chances that the trend has not become lateral but **bearish** increase. At this point, all our bullish (**Long**) positions should have already been closed.

However, the signals are not yet enough to assure us a start of a **Short Trend**.

We have certainty when the **Neckline** is also broken. The Neckline is a line we draw when we notice the existence of the head and the two shoulders. It passes through the lows at the base of the Head.

Usually, it is a line slightly inclined upwards or horizontal. **Volume** should increase with its break.

We can expect subsequently a small upward **movement**, an attempt to regain the levels just lost, which usually ends with a touch of the **Neckline** in its lower part.

### "Double and Triple Top/Bottom" Formations

With **Top** formations, volume usually decreases at each touch of the **resistance** and increases at the break of the **support**. The opposite with **Bottom** formation.

The figure represents the **Triple Top**. Also, the "Double Top/Bottom" is a formation we can find very often, equivalent but with only two Tops or two Bottoms.

Remember that the **structure** is confirmed only when it is broken: when support is lost in case of Top and when resistance is broken in case of Bottom.

### "Rounding Bottom and Spikes" Formations

These are rarer formations than the previous ones and certainly less predictable.

The **rounding bottom** we identify with a slow and gradual movement that goes to form a cup figure or, indeed, a U.

The **Spike** is what you see in the figure, a sudden movement characterized by **high volumes**. It is a typical reaction to important news, both negative and positive.

## Continuation Formations

**Continuation** formations indicate a pause in the ongoing Trend, usually of medium/short duration, and then a resumption of the previous movement.

The **main formations** are:

- Triangles
- Flags
- Pennants
- Wedges
- Rectangles

### Triangles

There are three types of **triangles**:

- symmetrical
- ascending
- descending

The **Symmetrical Triangle** has the upper line descending and the lower line ascending. If it forms after an uptrend it is bullish and vice versa.

The **ascending triangle** has the upper line horizontal and the lower line ascending. It is usually a formation that breaks **upwards**.

The **descending triangle** has the lower line flat and the upper line descending. It is usually **bearish**.

The distance between the first top and the first bottom of the triangle is about the movement expected once broken. At the **break**, a significant **increase in volumes** is also expected, usually low within the structure.

### Flags and Pennants

These formations are as common as they are **brief**. They can be both descending channels and triangles, it doesn't matter.

What differentiates them from other structures is their brevity: they always represent a **brief stop** before a restart of the main trend.

Therefore they have **volumes** and **volatility** much lower than other structures.

This is a **Bullish Flag**. If instead of a descending channel there had been a triangle it would be a **Bullish Pennant**.

### Wedge Formation

The **Wedge formation** is similar to the previous ones: it is a triangle with the only difference that both lines are opposite to the main trend.

In this case: uptrend, both lines descending.

### Rectangles

It is the figure par excellence of **sideways movement**. It is a rectangular zone where the price stalls for even a long period.

In this case, it is the study of **volume** that helps us understand if the price will reverse the trend and continue it.

A peculiarity of this situation is the possibility of exploiting the **volatility** of the price within the **range**.

## Volume Analysis and Open Interest (OI)

**Volume analysis** applies to every market, while **open interest** only to the **Futures** market.

Both are used as confirmation indicators of what we have already drawn from the price movement, which remains the main metric.

Volume is the number of securities/**assets**/contracts traded in a given period. It is used only for daily charts or even shorter.

Open Interest literally means "**Open Interest**". It means the number of contracts still open at the end of the day. It is calculated as the total of both Long and Short contracts.

It is useful to understand the **strength of the trend** in progress by checking if money is entering or leaving the market.

So a **low OI** does not mean that the price will fall, but indicates a **lower strength** of the Trend in progress, which can be either bullish or bearish.

In general, a **divergence** between the price trend and OI is a Trend reversal signal, because it indicates a beginning of **weakness** of it.

As you will have understood, they are two similar metrics.

## Moving Averages

Among all **technical indicators**, **moving averages** are the most used. Obviously, there are several types and they are very customizable based on the time frame we want to consider.

They are averages calculated on the last tot number of periods.

If for example we are in a **daily chart** and use a 10-period average, then the average will be calculated on the last 10 days.

So moving because each period shifts forward and calculates a different value.

A moving average, however, is different from the tools we have seen so far, it **does not anticipate the market** but can only follow it. This by mathematical definition.

In fact, the calculation of the average is done at the end of a period.

Precisely to obviate this nature, different moving averages have been created, which follow more recent price movements more closely. For example, the **exponential moving average**.

It is used a lot for the analysis of **Cryptocurrency** charts, being very volatile.

Although **moving averages** are lagging indicators, they can be used for **buy** and **sell** signals.

A classic use is that of a single moving average. With its overcoming, there is a buy signal and vice versa. Moving averages being **mathematical indicators** can also be used by **Trading Bots** in total automation.

Another method is to use two moving averages: one short-term (5-10 days for example) and one long-term (20-50 days).

The upward crossing of the faster average on the slower one is a buy signal (it means that the price is performing better in the last period), called in jargon **Golden Cross**.

The downward crossing is a sell signal, called **Death Cross**.

But in non-volatile market situations, averages do not perform as well as other indicators.

There is in particular a set of indicators that performs very well in those situations: **oscillators**.

## Oscillators

**Oscillators** are used especially in periods where the market is **trendless**. Not only though. Also in well-defined trend phases to identify phases of excess.

For example in a **Bullish** trend to understand when the price is **overbought** and could retrace.

They can also be useful to anticipate the end of a Trend. It is important to keep in mind, as for **moving averages**, that they are **secondary** indicators. Furthermore, there could be situations where they are not valid guides.

Oscillators have this representation:

The central line represents zero. High peaks are **overbought** situations and low ones **oversold**.

### Momentum

**Momentum** is one of the most used oscillators. It measures the **rate of change** of prices compared to their actual levels.

The formula for Momentum is: last closing price - price of X previous days. Usually, a 10-day interval is used.

The study of **Momentum** can be very useful to check a **Trend**. Let me explain better: it can give us important signals about its progress and its **strength**.

If prices are rising and Momentum too (movements are concordant), then the trend is accelerating.

When prices continue to rise but Momentum starts to fall, we have an important signal of loss of strength of the Trend. The opposite with a Downward Trend.

Although it is a **complicated** tool to use, especially because you have to understand the right moment, it is very precious as it manages to **anticipate** **the market**.

### RSI

This oscillator is very **popular** and is the improvement of the previous Momentum.

This does not mean that Momentum should not be used, because it will always remain valid, but **RSI** is a much **simpler** version to use.

It solves problems like erroneous movements that the previous one could make in particular market situations and also assigns a fixed vertical **range** between 0 and 100.

Although it might not interest you, I leave you the formula anyway in case you are curious:

**RSI = 100 - [100/(1+RS)]**

RS= average upward closings of x days / average downward closings of x days

The functioning is the usual: high zone (**>70**), overbought and low zone (**<30**), oversold.

**Trendlines** are very useful, as on the price chart. Breaks of them can be analyzed as in a price chart. This makes the use of RSI simpler than Momentum and preferable by many.
