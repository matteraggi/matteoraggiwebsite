## Why people don't like Bitcoin

Surely there are people who, by informing themselves about **Bitcoin**, have understood it. But they still don't appreciate it. That's fine.

But there are other people, the majority, who despise Bitcoin without ever having **informed** themselves.

So why is Bitcoin disliked?

1. It is a **currency** different from those we use every day, and what is different scares
2. Given the very **volatile** value, it ends up being associated more with trading than with its properties
3. It cannot be found in a **bank**, considered the safest place when it comes to money
4. "It has no **intrinsic** value"

Let's start from Point 1.

What is different scares us, it's not news. And being able to use a currency completely different from the **Euro** we are used to, doesn't seem like a great idea to many.

They are not even interested in possible alternatives to the Euro, they are fine as they are.

And it makes sense, but this thought can hide **financial** ignorance.

A lack of knowledge of information regarding the functioning of **FIAT** currencies and their depreciation.

In fact, I am sure that most people who make these statements are not even educated on **investments** in the stock market.

So the lack of knowledge of Bitcoin is a secondary problem.

Despite this, it is normal to be afraid of change. Especially now that **Bitcoin** is still not widely spread.

Point 2.

This is both a problem and a benefit for Bitcoin.

I'm talking about the speculation / **trading** factor.

When talking about Bitcoin there are usually two options.

One can discuss possible applications in everyday life or how it could be used as a **money** reserve.

And one can discuss how one can earn with its price fluctuations. How to trade exploiting **volatility**, and things like that.

But it is not a Bitcoin exclusive:

- One can talk about **Apple** products and its shares on the stock exchange
- One can use the Euro to pay or exchange it on forex markets (**Forex** Trading)

And this has certainly brought benefits: it has made Bitcoin more famous.

But on the other hand, it has made it **famous** for the "wrong things". So it is known mainly for being very volatile on the markets, rather than for its merits.

This can only be solved with a decrease in the volatility of the currency. So with an increase in adoption.

That's right, because **Bitcoin** is not destined to be volatile.

An Asset is volatile if it is poorly capitalized, so even a few movements can cause large price changes. Even **gold** was much more volatile once.

Point 3.

**Banks** have always been considered the core of money. And they certainly are for FIAT currencies.

The fact that Bitcoin does not rely on banks makes many turn up their noses. To people, it almost seems like a shady, **illegal** thing.

Just because it doesn't need banks. This point too is affected by underlying financial ignorance.

That is, the belief that relying on banks is a positive factor, when in reality the fact that one can exchange value bypassing their control is incredible.

On this statement, I could dwell for hours, there would be much to say.

But I will limit myself to saying this:

Banks are not a place where our money is **safe**. Or rather, it is certainly difficult for it to be stolen, but there are other problems.

When we put our money in the bank, it no longer belongs to us. We are **lending** it to them.

The bank in turn will lend it to others, that's how the **system** works.

You probably wouldn't be able to withdraw all your **money** from the bank at once. Precisely because it is lent to other people.

Point 4.

"Bitcoin has no **intrinsic value**"

This classic phrase distinguishes itself in two different meanings.

Those who say it comparing Bitcoin to **FIAT** currencies, considering it as an exchange currency.

And those who compare it to other investment **Assets**.

Let me say that the first case is truly absurd.

FIAT currencies, like all exchange currencies or stores of value DO NOT have intrinsic value.

Yes, gold has no intrinsic value. It is "simply" a **rare** asset, and as rare the supply/demand ratio could cause the price to rise over time. But obviously, it is not that simple.

The **dollar** and the euro, on the other hand, are the assets with the least intrinsic value in the world. And it is incredible how some people think the opposite.

The value of **FIAT** currencies does not exist. Indefinite quantities of banknotes are printed by central banks.

They are simply pieces of paper, accepted by everyone as a "**symbol of value**".

And, guess what, we chose the worst asset.

Choosing **Bitcoin** as a means of value exchange would certainly be better. This is due to its deflationary nature.

In fact, if gold is a rare asset, Bitcoin is a **very rare** asset. It is even limited (21 million).

This then leads to broader discussions regarding the characteristics a currency should have.

If instead we compare Bitcoin with **Stocks**, I understand the statement regarding intrinsic value.

Stocks have a precise value. Which is determined by what the company produces and **earns**. Therefore by its employees and its choices.

Bitcoin creates no product, and has no employees. Bitcoin does not earn.

But let's remember that Bitcoin offers a **service**, alternative to FIAT currencies. And this service is to allow people to have **real possession** of their own money (and much more).

Furthermore, we must remember that buying Bitcoin is different from buying stocks. Even if... not too much.

Buying Bitcoin (with the aim of obtaining a future profit) means **betting** that the Bitcoin service can be used more over time.

It sounds like a **gamble**, right? I used the term bet on purpose.

It doesn't seem like an investment.

But what are we doing when we go to buy shares of our beloved **Apple**?

We are betting on the fact that the company in the future can make more profit or in any case perform better on the **market**.

I see no differences between buying shares of a company on the stock exchange and buying **Bitcoin**. Especially if the company is less capitalized and is therefore volatile like Bitcoin.

In the end, they are both high-risk investments.

Instead, there is certainly a difference between investing in Bitcoin and buying shares of a global **ETF**.

Such a large and distributed index will always tend, barring major recessions, to grow in the **long term**.

In fact, in addition to recommending a **PAC** (Accumulation Plan) on a global ETF to anyone, I believe that these two types of **investment** should always be differentiated.

Two investments with very different risk levels.

- interesting but not revolutionary projects
- useless projects
- scams

All these projects will tend to disappear over time. And they will be replaced by other projects like them.
