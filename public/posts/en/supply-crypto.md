## Crypto Supply: How does it work?

Let's start from the beginning, that is from the **creation of Bitcoin**.

**Satoshi Nakamoto**, or the creator of Bitcoin, decided to build Bitcoin as a limited capitalization asset (**Hard Cap** it is said), placing this limit at 21 million.

But what are the reasons behind this choice?

The entire system has been balanced in such a way as to (assuming constant demand) increase the **value** of the Asset over time.

I specified "assuming constant demand" because the value of a rare asset is usually described through a **supply and demand** graph. So?

So if demand drops, even with a decreasing supply, the **price** of the asset can still go down.

There is a widely used chart that tries to hypothesize the future price of "**scarce**" assets.

I wrote "scarce" assets when actually it is not exactly like that. The model I am talking about is called **Stock to Flow** (S2F) and is applied to assets with a **supply** much lower in relation to that already present.

So these assets, like gold, can also be present in large quantities. But they are characterized by the fact that, annually or monthly, they are produced at a **scarce rate**.

We said that there will only be **21 million** Bitcoins. This threshold will be reached indicatively in 2140.

Currently, there are about 19 million **Bitcoins** in circulation but they are mined at a rate of 0.7 million each year. This rate is halved every 4 years.

That's what I meant by scarce rate. In relation to the total, what is put into **circulation** every year is of little relevance.

But does this **predictive** chart work?

For the moment it seems to have done its job.

As you can see, the value of **Bitcoin** never deviates much from the predictive line. And it always comes back to cross it.

However, this chart does not particularly take into account the **demand** for the asset, which is still fundamental.

If significant jumps in demand were to occur, both positive and negative, the price could deviate from the **S2F**.

## There is not just one Supply

In the **Crypto** world, there are many new terms, difficult to understand at first (PS: I wrote an article specifically: [Crypto Terminology](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/terminologia-crypto/)), and some of these concern Supply.

I've already explained what Supply is, but there are three types:

- **Total** Supply
- **Circulating** Supply
- **Maximum** Supply

Don't worry, they are not difficult terms to understand after what I explained throughout this article.

## Total Supply

Total Supply is the number of Cryptocurrencies that are in circulation (including those **locked** and reserved), therefore that have already been extracted, minus all burned or destroyed coins.

Yes, if you don't know, the number of coins can also be **reduced**, not just decrease the growth speed. But this is a whole other topic.

If you are interested in exploring it, I leave you a link to a valid article: [What is Cryptocurrency Burning](https://crypto.bernardomascellani.com/tips/cose-il-burning-di-criptovalute/).

## Circulating Supply

Circulating Supply is the total number of coins in **circulation** that are available **publicly**. So it does not include locked and reserved Cryptos which are included in the Total.

Basically, they are all those that can circulate on the market, as the name says.

## Maximum Supply

Maximum Supply is the sum of all **Crypto** that will ever be generated.

The **maximum** supply that a given Cryptocurrency can reach.
