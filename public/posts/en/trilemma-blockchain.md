The **Blockchain Trilemma**, a term coined for the first time by [**Vitalik Buterin** ](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitalik_Buterin)(creator of Ethereum), represents the real problem of this **technology**.

The problem consists in creating a **Blockchain** that is perfectly balanced between: **Decentralization**, **Security**, and **Scalability**.

Blockchain is an expanding **technology**, and it seems that for the moment there is no real **solution**.

One of the three points must be sacrificed. It is still not understood how to create a **perfect Blockchain**.

But is it really so?

Let's analyze every possible solution to the **Blockchain Trilemma**, but first let's try to understand what Scalability, Security, and Decentralization really are.

## What is Blockchain

**Blockchain** is the technology behind **Cryptocurrencies**, [**NFTs**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/nft/), **Smart Contracts**, and much more.

The fundamental characteristic of this technology is the ability to **verify data registries** and information in a secure, distributed, immutable, and open way.

Without the need to have a **centralized entity** controlling it.

The **Blockchain** therefore allows avoiding **intermediaries** in currency exchanges: banks, financial institutions, and others.

Information is secure because the Blockchain network is **distributed**. All **nodes** of the Blockchain must confirm the validity of operations.

Each node is a **computer** and each of them helps validate transactions.

This distribution of the **network** makes it extremely **secure** and impossible to sabotage or hack.

Furthermore, data of all transactions are obviously **public**.

It is difficult reading only these lines to understand the true **potential of Blockchain**. I'll show you some examples to give you a better idea.

[**Smart Contracts**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0-dapps/) are a development of this **technology** that is not talked about much but which are actually widely used.

They are **transparent**: so everyone can see the movements or details of the agreement.

## What is the Blockchain Trilemma

Let's finally talk about the **Blockchain Trilemma**.

We understood what this technology is and what it is capable of letting us do, but the **Trilemma** highlights its great weak point.

As mentioned in the preface, this term was coined by the creator of Ethereum: **Vitalik Buterin**.

He himself found himself facing this problem with the creation of his **Cryptocurrency**. And actually, he is still facing it now.

This is because we have not managed for the moment to obtain a real definitive **solution**.

The **Blockchain Trilemma** divides the main characteristics of this technology into three parts:

- **Scalability**
- **Security**
- **Decentralization**

These are the three peculiarities that a **Blockchain network** must provide.

But it is not that simple. Indeed, it is rather complicated.

A secure and **decentralized** network like Bitcoin is not very **scalable**.

A very **scalable** and secure network is not **decentralized**.

It seems impossible to find the right **balance** between these three values. Even if perhaps **solutions** are emerging.

But before seeing them, it is better to understand the three points of the **Blockchain Trilemma** well.

## Blockchain Trilemma: Scalability

What is the **scalability of a network**?

Scalability is the ability of a **Blockchain** to sustain a high number of **transactions** without suffering in terms of **times** and **costs**.

The search for decentralization in **Cryptocurrencies** often makes it difficult to manage many transactions in a short time, this is because the **verification process** is slow.

### Scalability problems

The main problems encountered are: **slowness** of transactions, low **throughput**, and high **fees**.

#### Transaction slowness

The **slowness** of transactions in a network occurs due to its strong **decentralization**.

Especially networks that rely on a transaction verification system of type [**Proof of Work (PoW)**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/terminologia-crypto/), like **Bitcoin**, are very slow.

All the most recent **Cryptocurrencies** are by now adopting a [**Proof of Stake (PoS)**](https://www.tuttocrypto.it/proof-of-stake-pos/) system, which is faster and also less expensive from the fees **point** of view.

#### Low Throughput

**Throughput** is the transaction measurement system. Its unit of measurement is **tps**, transactions per second.

It serves to measure the **speed** of a network: when the transaction limit is exceeded, it is forced to put them in a **queue**. This obviously causes large **waiting** times.

**PoW** networks are the slowest.

**Bitcoin** has an average speed of 3.5 tps, instead [Ripple](https://ripple.com) which is the fastest reaches 1500 tps. But we will return to this comparison later.

#### High Fees

This last point connects to the others. Due to the previous points, the network suffers **cost** increases.

**Miners**, those who offer computing power to support a Proof of Work **Blockchain**, must be rewarded. Slower transactions, more **expensive** transactions.

## The scalability of Bitcoin and other crypto

**Bitcoin** has terrible scalability. Let's take the image of the **Blockchain Trilemma**.



Bitcoin is an extremely **decentralized** and very secure **Crypto**. This as you can see means **poor scalability**.

Unfortunately, it has a maximum speed of only 7 tps, and an average of 3.5.

Also for this reason, it is considered by many more a **store of value** than an exchange currency. To be used for few large movements.

**Bitcoin's** problem comes from the limit that was imposed on its **blocks** when it was programmed.

They can reach the maximum size of **1Mb**.

This choice was made to avoid **hacker** attacks, which if there had not been a limit could have congested the network by sending enormous **blocks**.

However, it also brings negative sides: with this limit the maximum **transactions per second** that Bitcoin can operate, which is really low.

Let's go back to the comparison from before: **Bitcoin** and **Ripple**.

I decided to compare Bitcoin with Ripple because it is among the **fastest** and most **capitalized** **Cryptocurrencies**.

Is Ripple better than Bitcoin? What does Ripple have that Bitcoin doesn't?

The truth is that there is no secret.

Ripple in fact manages to have a high **transaction speed** only because another pillar of the **Blockchain Trilemma** is missing.

The most important of all and the characteristic point of cryptocurrencies: **decentralization**.

The "**Cryptocurrency**" **Ripple** is actually more similar to a centralized bank.

It is completely **centralized** and the coins are not even your property, but belong to **Ripple laboratories**.

So be careful, because this system is exactly like that of a centralized entity, the values with which **Crypto** were born are missing.

## Blockchain Trilemma: Security

**Security** is a strong point of the **Blockchain** network and if it is used in many fields many times it is precisely for this reason.

Having a **digital** technology that manages to guarantee complete **data** security is incredible.

Blockchain is **decentralized** so taking possession of 51% of the network is almost **impossible** (the possibility of making a move of this type would severely affect security).

Usually, **security** being implicit in the technology, this is the point that loses the least. Even if an increase in Scalability can decrease it.

In fact, even if the **blockchain trilemma** is composed of three points, the real problem is between: **Scalability** and **Security-Decentralization**, which oppose each other.

## Blockchain Trilemma: Decentralization

**Decentralization** is the reason why **Blockchain** was created.

A network is decentralized when control is not on a single entity or on a few people but on a large number of **users**, all on the **same level**.

Who is the "boss" of Bitcoin? Who controls Bitcoin?

**No one**. Every person who uses or supports the network is on the same level and has the **same power**.

This means **decentralization**, there is no one who can decide to close the network from one day to the next or change its rules.

It is fundamental that a **Cryptocurrency** is **decentralized** and it is the point that most of all cannot be missing.

### Problem of decentralization

The main problem of maintaining **high Decentralization** is the relationship it must have with **scalability**.

Solutions are being sought to improve network scalability without affecting decentralization.

Which must not be damaged in any way.

The more **processors** participate in the network, the more it is **decentralized** and **slow**.

Fewer but very powerful processors increase **speed** and therefore **scalability**. But a network composed of few processors **is not a decentralized network**.

### No Proof of Work, yes Proof of Stake

A **solution** that seems to bring mostly advantages is the change of [**Consensus Algorithm**](https://www.mrwoland.it/cose-un-algoritmo-di-consenso/).

**Proof of Work (PoW)** is a consensus algorithm based on solving particularly complex **mathematical algorithms**.

Solving requires the use of a lot of **distributed computing power**.

Anyone can provide their own computer or use processors. If you employ this computing power to solve them it means you are **mining**.

**Miners** are those who carry out this activity ([**Mining**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/terminologia-crypto/)) and are rewarded with a certain amount of the **Cryptocurrency** in question.

The resolution of these problems serves to validate **network transactions**.

The **Proof of Stake (PoS)** algorithm instead works differently.

It was born after **PoW** and is increasingly taking hold. Most **Altcoins** use this system. **Ethereum** included.

**Proof of Stake** is created to overcome the problems of its predecessor: slowness of **block verification**, cost of required electricity, and transaction costs.

**Staking** is a much less complex process: you lock a chosen amount of **Cryptocurrencies** and get rewarded with a **percentage** on the amount that has been put in **stake**.

This type of algorithm makes the network much more **scalable** maintaining security and decentralization.

### Sharding

The word **Sharding** comes from **Shard** which means fragment. A sharding operation is a **fractionation** operation of a [**Database**](https://www.informaticapertutti.com/che-cose-un-database/) (information archive).

The fractionated **Databases** that will be created will be able to manage the **network** much faster (increase in scalability) without decreasing its decentralization.

It is much simpler to manage a **fractionated Database** than a whole one, even **management costs** decrease.

The doubt in this case is on **security**.

There could be the risk of an attack on a single **fragment** (shard).
To take a shard much **fewer resources** will be needed than to take the non-fractionated network.

In fact, to attack the entire network, **51%** of the total power or total staking would be needed. To take a **fragment**, much less is needed.

Assigning the **shard** randomly can prevent this type of attack, but we still do not know for sure if **Sharding** can be a good solution for the **Blockchain Trilemma**.

### State Channel (Lighting Network)

From this moment on, the solutions will use [**Layer 2**](https://www.mynecrypto.com/cosa-significa-layer-1-e-layer-2/): protocols that rely on the already existing **Blockchain**.

The possibility of building **layer 2** allows speeding up the advancement of this technology and promoting **development**.

This possibility is one of the features I like most about this world.

**State Channels** lighten the load on the main **Blockchain**, moving it to a second layer.

In this way, **scalability** problems of the main network are avoided.

Without getting too technical there is an example I can give you.

This among all is one of the solutions used most to solve the **Blockchain Trilemma**.

The most used State Channel is the [**Lighting Network**](https://lightning.network), Layer 2 of **Bitcoin**.

It serves to manage **transactions** outside the **main network**. It manages to execute them very quickly and with very low costs.

And there is no loss in any of the other two points of the **Blockchain Trilemma**.

The way it does it is quite complicated but basically, the only transactions of a block recorded on **Layer 1** are the first and the last.

With the use of the **Lighting Network**, **Bitcoin's** tps can reach almost a million.

### SideChain

The idea is to create **secondary networks** that interact as little as possible with the main one so as not to burden it.

The **main network** maintains in a contract links with the secondary one and a proof of the **current state** of the chain.

The **secondary network** instead ensures that only **valid transactions** are sent to the **primary network**.

### Collaborations between Blockchains (Relay Chain)

**Relay Chains** are used by the Cryptocurrency [**Polkadot**](https://polkadot.network), which makes it its strong point.

**Relay Chain** technology allows building a **network of networks**. The main network (Relay Chain) and secondary networks are all connected under the same **network**.

The **Polkadot** main network imposes no restrictions on what **secondary networks** can do, but offers a shared **network** in which they can develop.

### Nested Blockchains

Nested **Blockchains** are layered or leveled networks.

Besides the **parent** layer, other "child" layers can be created. They can carry out the work delegated by the **main network**.

While the main network never influences **lower levels**.
