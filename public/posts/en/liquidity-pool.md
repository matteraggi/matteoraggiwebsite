## The definition of Liquidity Pool.

Liquidity Pools are the tool used in DeFi to allow Trading and loans.

They are a deposit of funds locked through a Smart Contract.

What is a Smart Contract?

Simply put, it is an intelligent contract. Intelligent because created through programming languages that make it automatic.

Without the need for intermediaries during the duration of the contract, a Smart Contract executes the task for which it was created.

Think about it, this fully reflects what Decentralized Finance aims to be.

An alternative not only cheaper than the centralized market, but also free from government power. And free from external intermediaries.

Therefore from other people with power over our assets.

Liquidity Pools work autonomously. And, precisely, completely decentralized.

## Liquidity Pools in detail.

There are two existing ways to operate on the market. The classic one is the order-book.

It is the simplest and most used. It connects buyer and seller who agree on a specific price.

Once an agreement on the price is found, the exchange is made.

So a classic Order book looks like a list of open orders ready to be satisfied once the counterparty is found.

How long does it take for orders to be satisfied?

For almost all the best-known markets, they are carried out in zero time.

This type of money management is efficient only if there is enough liquidity in the market.

Only in this way can purchases and sales be made quickly.

For pairs with scarce liquidity, the best solution is another one.

This second method for exchanging currencies on the market uses Liquidity Pools.

These are Automatic Market Makers (AMM). Which, drum roll, do not need an order book.

Instead, they use Smart Contracts, which I already mentioned in the previous paragraph.

They automatically create Liquidity Pools that will then serve to fuel exchanges.

This solution is usually better in Decentralized markets also because it involves lower costs.

But what are these AMMs (Automatic Market Makers) really?

AMMs bring market exchanges from a classic peer-to-peer system to a peer-to-order system.

Peer-to-peer means user to user.

This happens, as I already told you, with order books. The exchange is made when the two users want to sell/buy at the same price.

A peer-to-contract system instead leads the user to exchange with a contract.

That is why it is a better system for less liquid pairs.

In many cases waiting for a user who desires the same price can be inefficient.

For this reason, Smart Contracts are a winning solution.

How do they work exactly?

Simple, a Smart Contract is exploited. And what happens in practice is that liquidity is drawn from the Liquidity Pool.

Managed precisely by automatic Smart Contracts.

Well, let's stop for a moment.

At this point in reading, a doubt would come to me: how is the Liquidity Pool filled?

It is time to introduce you to Liquidity Providers.

They provide liquidity and in exchange are rewarded in the form of Tokens. They earn with Trading fees.

How is the price of Assets decided instead?

It is very interesting. It is based on the ratio between the reserves of the two currencies in the Pool.

Let's take for example the ETH/UNI exchange.

If Ethereum reserves increase in the Pool, the price moves in favor of the Uniswap token. And vice versa.

In this way, the value is regulated solely based on what is present in the Liquidity Pool.

## How to exchange Crypto through Liquidity Pools

Usage is not very complex but there is an important entry barrier that separates most Crypto users from Liquidity Pools.

And that is DeFi.

DeFi stands for Decentralized Finance.

Why is DeFi a big entry barrier?

Decentralized Finance uses decentralized platforms. So not the usual Exchanges like Binance or Coinbase.

But Decentralized Exchanges, which truly few people are interested in using.

Examples of Decentralized Exchanges?

Uniswap, Sushiswap, and Curve.

Names you might not have heard, or that in any case resonate less than the names mentioned previously.

That said, how are Liquidity Pools of Decentralized Exchanges used?

Well, actually the procedure is identical to that of classic Exchanges.

You understood correctly, absolutely nothing changes.

You won't even realize you are using a Liquidity Pool, because it is just another method to manage Cryptocurrency exchanges.

But these exchanges, from the user's point of view, work in exactly the same way.

This is also an important incentive for those who are scared of using it.

## How to provide Liquidity to Liquidity Pools

The procedure is not complex at all and is similar to other passive income procedures.

Like Staking for example.

In fact, the steps are the following:

- Deposit Assets into a Liquidity Pool
- Receive reward Tokens
- end

Very simple right?

To deposit Assets just go to the correct section of the chosen exchange.

## Pros and Cons.

Pros:

- Shorter times for less liquid exchange pairs
- Passive and automatic intermediation
- Anyone can earn by providing liquidity
- Fewer fees

Cons:

- Temporary losses can happen due to market fluctuations
- There may be bugs in smart contracts (difficult in widely used platforms)
