## After Web 1.0: what are the changes of Web 2.0

**Web 2.0** or **Dynamic Web** is the subsequent evolution of [Web 1.0](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-1-0/). It was instead called **Static Web** due to the lack of "dialogue" between administrators and users.

Only administrators had the ability to modify information, disseminate it, and sell online. Users were merely consumers.

The great change brought about by this evolution is indeed fully represented by the adjective attributed to the Web: "**Dynamic**".

Currently, we are still using it, finding ourselves in a transition phase with [**Web 3.0**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0/).

To better understand what it is, it is good to specify that we are not talking about a single technology or a creation of a single person.

The dynamic Web is the union of all the **technological developments** that followed the creation of **web 1.0**. A slow process, lasting years, thus allowed **Internet** to acquire all the functionalities that made it "2.0".

Internet pages were no longer built statically, unable to change beyond that of the administrator, but were made **dynamic**.

This means that the user could also access **information**, which interacted actively with them and responded accordingly.

The dynamic Web creates all **web applications**, thus evolved Websites such as: Forums, Blog Comments, Search Engines, and E-commerce.

Practical examples of **web 2.0** are: Instagram, Facebook, [WordPress](http://wordpress.com), YouTube, [Linkedin](http://linkedin.com), and many others. Trivially, all **social networks** belong to it, where, unlike web 1.0, the user comes first.

Social networks are indeed the full realization of **web 2.0**. The entire operation is based on the interaction of users with each other, and the creation of content accessible to everyone.

For the first time, it is not companies but individuals who create the content that populates the **Internet**, and everyone can be part of it.

This enormous possibility gave the Web the ability to become **public domain**, and used by a large part of the world's population.

## The user commands in web 2.0

Before, the **Internet** was used only by the military and universities, then it was used by companies and a few consumer users, then over the years it became increasingly **decentralized** until it was used by most human beings.

The process of decentralization of the web began years ago and is still continuing. With [**Web 3.0**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0/) it will be at an even higher level with **[Blockchain](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/criptovalute-sono-il-futuro/)**, but what does this mean for us?

The decentralization of the Web allows every user to carve out more space in this world, parallel to the real one.

In **Web 2.0**, this process allowed users not just to watch, but also to **create content**.

Thus began the birth of [**Blogs**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blog), Web spaces dedicated to a single user, free to use it to communicate whatever they wanted.

Then Youtube, then Facebook, then Instagram, and so on.

The user now has the possibility and ability to **carve out their own place on the Internet**, something that was not feasible before this "revolution".

What is important to understand about these **changes** of the Web is that they happen over **very long periods**, just think that the first Blog was opened in 1997, while Facebook was created in 2004, and Instagram in 2010. And we are always talking about **web 2.0**.

## The dispersiveness of Web 2.0

**More users, more content, more dispersiveness**.

I talked about how everyone today can propose their content on the web, and it is true, but it is also increasingly **difficult to get noticed** and obtain one's own space because competitiveness continues to grow.

Despite this, every human being with the right spirit of will can manage to find their audience, having all the tools available.

So if there were an expression that could describe Web 2.0, that would be: **"Web-user interaction"**.

The magnitude of such an evolution is unimaginable. It opened the door to an countless amount of **new online jobs**, which are still increasing and becoming more numerous.

**Social networks** like Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook, but especially YouTube and Websites, have generated millions of jobs and earning methods within everyone's reach.

On the Internet, you can **build yourself**, you can learn anything with your computer and you can also sell anything.

**Web 2.0** has also opened the door to **advertising**, "exploiting" user content.

## The landing of online advertising

The **Dynamic Web** brought to the Internet the expansion of **advertising**, which was previously present only in the real world or on television.

The importance of this arrival is unimaginable, a large part of **online businesses** is based precisely on advertising.

First of all, it is divided into two areas: advertising provided by the **platform** and **company-content creator** advertising.

The former are very important and are for example the ads you always see between YouTube videos and among the pages of many Websites.

These allow monthly income for the creators of the content you are viewing.

If this type of advertising were missing, content creators would not be encouraged to create them and there would be many fewer videos and many fewer internet pages.

Basically, the Web would not be a place like the one we know today, but it would be **empty**.

That is why it is very important to **reward Internet users** who provide other people with content that is appreciated and viewed.

The second type of advertising does not have the platform as an intermediary and is not as revolutionary as the first type. It is simply companies collaborating with public figures to make the product known.

This practice was already common outside the Web world, but the **possibility of doing it on the Internet too** expanded the possibilities.

## Web 2.0: an answer to everything always

The last **great innovation** I want to report is one of the most important in the daily life of every person, whether they work online or not.

It includes [**search engines**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/motore-di-ricerca-cos-e/) and **web search**. With the static Web, once you entered the Internet you couldn't search for what you wanted, but only "explore" what pages were present.

With Web 2.0 and the arrival of search engines like Google, every person could **search for any information** they wanted.

The integration of search engines with the ability for every user to create content and earn from it creates a combination that never existed before.

A real **digital encyclopedia** was born, at the service of everyone and much more complete than a classic physical encyclopedia.

Updated 24/7 and with answers to any question and any problem.

I believe this change is among the most significant in the history of humanity. I myself do at least 10 searches every day on **Google**.

Whether it's places to eat, where to go on vacation, the meaning of a word, or anything else, we always have Google available.

**Here is what web 2.0 is**.
