## The history of the Web: from web 1.0 to web 3.0

### Web 1.0

**Web 3.0** is the latest evolution of the Web. Its first version was [**Web 1.0**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-1-0/) and allowed users only to view content, without any possibility of **interaction**.

It was top-down communication: **Internet** users were not all on the same level and few could actively interact and propose content.

It was usually actively used only by **large companies** to put products on sale.

The real problem of this first version was its maximum **centralization**, without communication between **web** modifiers and normal **users**.

The Web was similar to a catalog of Websites, with which one could not interact but **only watch**.

It was not a place, like today, where people can meet to **communicate remotely**, it was more like a simple list of Companies with their "digital flyers".

The functions that existed were not so revolutionary, and no one could imagine what the internet would become in the following years.

I recommend you watch this part of the **1995 interview** regarding the Web between **Bill Gates** and **David Letterman**.

What this video conveys is very important and makes one understand how technology can **evolve** in an **unexpected** way and also how important it can be to keep an **open mind to changes**.

So we cannot know if now all the evolutions of Web 3.0, **NFTs** and **Cryptocurrencies** in particular, are incredible and comparable to what we saw in the video, but there is a **possibility** that they are.

I talk about Web 1.0 more thoroughly in the dedicated article: [Web 1.0: the ancestor of today's Internet](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-1-0/).

### Web 2.0

With the second version, precisely this interaction increased significantly.

Bringing all those **online applications** that allow people to interact with each other and with companies: for example **social** **networks** and **blogs**.

Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, Google, allow users to post freely and produce **content** **with the web**, interacting also with companies through **advertising** or **sponsorships.**

[**Web 2.0**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-2-0/) thus opens a new frontier of **profit**: every content became free for the user and this made the internet a place **accessible to anyone**, but above all it allowed every creator, even non-expert, to earn through content production, then "exploited" by companies through advertising.

This system, composed of **affiliations**, **advertising**, and **sponsorships** created a new type of capitalism on the web: it was an incredible revolution because it allowed companies, creators, and users to reach a winning solution for everyone.

## Artificial Intelligence

[**Artificial Intelligence**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/intelligenza-artificiale-nella-vita-quotidiana/) in web 3.0 will be used in **intelligent search**: through an understanding of contents by the web instead of a simple categorization.

It will be able to understand what the user wants not only through keywords but because it will manage to **comprehend** what they are looking for.

This will allow the Web to provide the user performing a search with a service of **much higher quality**.

Instead of proposing a series of **results** on the [search engine](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/motore-di-ricerca-cos-e/), it will be able to **understand them** and use them to build an even more complete and valid product, but above all 100% **relevant** to the specific **search**.

## Machine Learning

**Machine Learning** is part of Artificial Intelligence and deals with creating systems that can **learn** and improve over time.

Basically an operation **similar to human beings**, so intelligent as to be able to process data to improve their **performance**.

It is a very advanced type of technology and which is now tended to be used almost always, practically all Artificial Intelligence systems use it.

From the programmer's point of view, this happens thanks to the use of **probability** and statistics, which serve the **algorithms** used to understand each time what is the best solution through **data analysis**.

Just like artificial intelligence in general, Machine Learning can **simplify** our life and especially corporate work, because it allows working on **large amounts of data** much more easily.

## Internet of Things

The [**Internet of Things**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/linternet-delle-cose/) (IOT) is the definition of the **web 3.0** process of merging the two worlds: the **Metaverse** (the virtual one) and the real world.

Simply put, it is the process that leads all commonly used objects to be interconnected with each other thanks to the internet.

Such as **virtual** **assistants** (google home/amazon echo) that can connect to light bulbs, televisions, and other objects in the house, creating precisely an **ecosystem** connected thanks to the web.

All these objects are called **Smart Objects** and are able to interact with each other.

Further into the future virtual and real objects can be united with an even tighter bond.

Thanks to the use of a technology similar to **3D printers** but much more evolved, it will be possible to **download the project** of an object from the internet and **produce it** in a few minutes.

The project will be read and executed by the printer which will **create** it for us, avoiding **waste of time** to go and buy it and enormously reducing **production expense**.

## What are Smart Contracts

**Smart Contracts** are not a recent discovery, but with Blockchain technology they have expressed their true potential. They are to be understood as "**event contracts**", because most of the time they are used as an if-then function: "if a certain thing happens or if something expires, then the payment starts".

Their true potential came out with the Blockchain precisely because, being **decentralized**, one of its strengths is precisely that it works without any control or decision by human beings.

A system was needed that could verify 100% the possession of something and that was reliable. The Blockchain was also born for this: a public technology that cannot deceive or be deceived.

Let me give you an example: a travel insurance company reimburses your ferry ticket if it is delayed by more than 3h.

Then thanks to a **smart contract**, without having to call assistance to ask for a refund and without having to rely on the choice of other people, the system **will verify the possession** of your ticket, the ferry delay, and will send you a refund automatically.

Smart contracts are also part of web 3.0.

## What are DApps

[**DApps**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0-dapps/), decentralized applications, rely on Blockchain technology and their **network**. This allows them to be resistant, because they will not be managed by **centralized servers**, immutable, because creators will not be able to have either possession or decision-making power of the platform once created and launched, and moreover will allow users to own their **personal data**. Using a centralized service your personal data comes into the possession of the platform, with DApps no.

There are already **Decentralized Exchanges** (Idex, Bancor, ...), games (CryptoKitties, EOS Knight, ...) and advertising platforms but also much more.
