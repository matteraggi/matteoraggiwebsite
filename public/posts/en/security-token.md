There are a multitude of **Tokens** all with different names and purposes: Security Tokens, Utility Tokens, Payment Tokens, Governance Tokens, AI Tokens, GameFi Tokens, Storage Tokens, and many others.

In this article, we analyze what **Security Tokens** are and what their utility is.

But first, let's quickly review **what a Token is**.

## What is a Token?

The name Token could be replaced by names like "**Chip**" or "**Coin**", which always indicate the same thing.

In the Cryptocurrency world, it can be defined as any fraction of a specific **Crypto**. They are therefore exchanged. In this sense, the name Coin conveys the idea much better.

But there are also different types from those I just described to you; in fact, a token may not even have its own **Blockchain**.

Let me explain better now.

The tokens I just described to you are fractions of a proprietary **Cryptocurrency**, such as **Satoshi** (fractions of Bitcoin).

However, through the use of **Smart Contracts** on platforms like Ethereum, Tokens different from the "proprietary" Crypto can be created but still based on the same Blockchain.

Using the ledger of another Cryptocurrency, anyone can create their own Token in a few minutes and without needing to build another network.

They take the name of Token.

This Coin will have the same characteristics as a Cryptocurrency, as it is based on the same technology, but it will not be native and internal to the platform. It will only use it as a base to record transactions.

They therefore represent a counterpart on Blockchain of something external.

From this operation comes the verb "to tokenize" which consists of creating a counterpart on Blockchain of an external asset, thanks to Smart Contracts.

Anything is Tokenizable.

The Ethereum network is the network that hosts the largest number of Tokens (called ERC-20), far ahead of the others. And these tokens can be of any type and function. The Ethereum network can literally be used to create anything.

Let's analyze Security Tokens in particular.

## What are Security Tokens

Security Tokens are Tokens linked to a particular financial product. Securities are in fact investment contracts that allow obtaining a return, a financial profit.

These Securities can be any type of Asset such as: Stocks, Bonds, collectibles, real estate, or other.

A Security Token therefore represents a fractional part of an Asset.

The operation might seem identical to the classic sale of Stocks through a broker and in some ways it is.

Tokenization, however, can remove many obstacles, facilitate fractionalization, give more flexibility in offering securities, and reduce costs.

Security tokens still guarantee owners their rights, such as dividends.

Furthermore, being in the form of Tokens, they can be launched under ICO (Initial Coin Offering), which in this case are called STO (Security Token Offering), instead of IPO (Initial Public Offering).

This implies that Security Tokens must face a series of issues and legislation superior to all other Tokens.

### Features and Howey Test

Security Tokens therefore follow the trend of the Asset in question, just like a real company stock.

Moreover, security is high thanks to the regulation provided by the Smart Contract on Blockchain.

In fact, they must comply with the Howey Test, both investors and companies.

The Howey Test serves to define if something is an investment contract. This is done through a series of parameters:

- it must be an investment of money
- the investment must be in a common enterprise
- there must be an expectation of profit
- this profit must be derived from the efforts of others

A first important category consists of Exchange Tokens. They are actually hybrids, a cross between Security Tokens and Utility Tokens (tokens that play an operational role in the platform). The main ones are:

## Differences with Utility Tokens

Utility Tokens are created to perform a specific role within the platform, so the user is driven to purchase thanks to the features they could access.

Security Tokens are instead a direct link with an Asset, so the user buys it if interested in obtaining it, as for the purchase of a stock.

Consequently, an important difference is given by the fact that the trend of the first is absolutely not correlated with the company's valuation.

A difference that cannot be overlooked is the difference from a regulatory point of view.

Security Tokens are regulated and subjected to the same controls existing for stock exchange sales, so they are secure.

Utility Tokens do not have this certainty. The best way to make you understand how insecure they are is to bring you back to the FTX event.

Not being regulated, it is not impossible to come across insecure platforms like FTX. After the [scandal](https://cryptonomist.ch/2022/11/26/vicenda-ftx-cosa-e-successo/) here is what happened to its FTT Token.

## Difference STO vs IPO

STO and IPO have the exact same purpose: purchasing an Asset at launch with the goal of seeing capital growth over time.

They might seem the same, but they differ greatly if we consider: technology, involved parties, structure, and costs.

IPOs in fact involve many intermediaries and secondary factors, all involved with the aim of receiving part of a company. But the most important factor is that all these involved parties create a huge barrier to entry for medium-small companies.

The IPO therefore becomes a valid option only for already very large companies and is starting to be an antiquated solution.

STOs are to all intents and purposes a modernization of IPOs.

Everything that IPOs do is also done by STOs, but the latter have additional advantages: they eliminate intermediaries, eliminate costs, and greatly reduce the barrier to entry allowing even Startups to take part.

## FAQ

**What is meant by security token?**

Security Tokens are Tokens linked to a particular financial product, which can be a stock, bonds, or even real estate.

**What are tokens used for?**

Tokens represent the ownership of an Asset or an object. They manage to do so securely thanks to the Blockchain. Owning a Token therefore means having ownership rights to that specific Asset.

**What can be done with a token?**

There are countless types of Tokens, each with a different purpose. Even if they have totally different functionalities, they can all be held in a digital wallet, like any Cryptocurrency.
