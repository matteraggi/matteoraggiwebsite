**Mining** is one of the many methods to earn with **Cryptocurrencies**. If you have a good budget available, it can be a viable option.

Usually, it remains an option intended for **large mining companies**. By now, there are quite a few of them.

But it may not necessarily be so; it certainly depends on many factors.

Some countries or places can be favorable for **mining**.

Usually, the characteristics must be: reduced **electricity** price and the lowest possible **temperature**.



The reduced electricity price obviously helps because it decreases the **expenses** that **mining** entails. Usually, they are absolutely not low.

While **low temperatures** allow for the natural cooling of all processors.

For this reason, **mining systems** are usually installed underground.

**Mining** can therefore be an attractive option, but it can be done on multiple **Cryptocurrencies**.

And among all of them, does it make sense to **mine Dogecoin**?

## What is Mining in short

**Mining** is one of the methods used to generate **Cryptocurrencies**.

[Proof of Work](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/terminologia-crypto/) **Crypto** use **Mining** precisely to issue currency.

Those using **Proof of Stake** use another method. If you want to deepen your understanding, read this article where I talk about it: [What is the Blockchain Trilemma? The 6 best solutions.](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/trilemma-blockchain/)

In addition to this function, **Mining** also serves to verify the validity of **transactions** made on the **network**.



But what does **Mining** consist of?

**Mining** takes place through a process of **decrypting very complex algorithms**. This process is carried out by very powerful **computers**.

The **processor** uses its energy to solve these **algorithms**, heats up, and consumes power. Just like when you use the computer for heavy tasks like **video editing** or **video games**. Only in this case, the work is even more complicated and is **24/7**.

Precisely for this reason, **Mining** consumes a lot of **electric current**, which serves to power the computers that perform this work.

Once the mathematical problem is **encrypted** (solved), a transaction is verified and thus a **block** is added to the network.

The block certifies that the transaction that occurred is **secure** and **certified**, and can no longer be modified.

The **Miners** who own the computers receive **cryptocurrencies** in exchange for the work done.

So here is how **Mining** performs its two main functions: **verification** and **distribution.**

## What is needed to do Mining

Before understanding if it makes sense to **Mine Dogecoin**, it is important to understand what expenses you will incur if you decide to do so.



1. **Cheap electricity plan**
   It is necessary to have an economic plan, so as not to risk cutting into every profit.
2. **Cold location**
   For the same reason described above, a cold place can help keep expenses lower.
3. **Internet Connection**
   You will need to have an Internet connection, so if you decide to create an underground mining station you will also need to connect it with an Ethernet cable.
4. [**Crypto Wallet**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/metamask/)
   You will obviously need a wallet to hold the Crypto you obtain.
5. **Dedicated Hardware**
   In the case of Dogecoin, GPU and ASIC types. These are graphics cards or CPUs that can cost thousands of euros.
6. **Specific Software**
   For example [EasyMiner](https://www.easyminer.net) or [CGMiner](https://cgminer.info).

## Does it make sense to Mine Dogecoin?

**Mining Dogecoin** is decidedly simpler than [**Mining Bitcoin**](https://focuscrescita.it/minare-bitcoin/). The required **computing power** is far inferior.

The discussion regarding the value of the currency is negligible from the point of view of **mining** since all the **Crypto** you earn can be converted into euros (or dollars).

So the answer is yes, **Dogecoin Mining** makes sense compared to other **Cryptocurrencies**.

But it only makes sense in some cases, which are common to all types of **Mining**.

You must indeed be careful about the **income-expense ratio** which might not be favorable depending on your electricity plan and the average temperature of the place you will have available.

To calculate the profits you can generate, you can use a **profit calculator** like this one from [CoinWarz.](https://www.coinwarz.com/mining/dogecoin/calculator)

You will have to set your **Hashrate** (your computing power), your power consumption, the cost of electricity, and any **mining** fees, which shouldn't be there.

## Alternative mining methods

Many recommend joining a **Mining Pool**, that is, a group of people with your same goal of **Mining the Crypto** in question.



**Computing power** is pooled together and mined as a single unit.

The problem is that usually unpleasant situations are created where there is **competition** within the same **pool**.

The problem becomes having sufficient **computing power** to be a good percentage of the Pool.

The best solution remains, in my opinion, at least for **Dogecoin**, to **Mine individually**.
