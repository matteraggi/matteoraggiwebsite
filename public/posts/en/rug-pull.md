The **Rug Pull** is, in the **crypto** world, a scam in which the creators of a **Token** release it with the sole purpose of selling everything and running away with the profits, scamming all the **investors**.

But don't worry, it's not that complicated to save yourself from this **trap**, just know the risks and pay attention to certain factors that we will now discuss.

## Rug Pull? What is it and Squid Game Token

The term **Rug Pull** means pulling the rug out from under your feet. The reason for this name I think is clear.

The creators of the project "pull the rug" out from under the feet of **investors** by taking all the **tokens** off the market and making all the unlucky ones "fall to the ground".

From one moment to the next, all **investments** on the specific **token** will be worth ZERO.

Maybe you've already heard of **Rug Pull**, probably due to the now renowned "**Squid Game Token**" event.

If you don't know it, I'll show you the chart immediately, to make you understand what actually happens with a **Rug Pull**.

It is difficult to notice the **spike** that the price had because of how fast it was.

The **Token** exploded in a short time exploiting the publicity given by the homonymous TV series. And after the rapid price rise... **Rug Pull**.

The **creators** sell everything and the token reaches a price close to **zero**.

In all, about 2.1 **million** dollars were "**stolen**" with this method.

## How does a Rug Pull happen?

I start by making one thing clear: if you are not a user of **Decentralized Exchanges** you probably will never risk suffering a **Rug Pull**.

This is because all **Centralized Exchanges** check and ensure the validity of a **Token** before listing it on the platform.

Clearly, it also depends on the **Exchange**: **Binance** has many listed tokens while **Coinbase** has many fewer.

But it is still difficult for a **crypto** listed on these Exchanges, even in those richest in **tokens**, to turn out to be a Rug Pull attempt.

Decentralized Exchanges (**DEX**) have much less security from this point of view. They are not less secure places, but being **decentralized** platforms they allow anyone to put their creations on sale.

Similar to **Dogecoin**, inspired by TV series, trending events, or characters with high visibility.

## Slow Rug Pulls

There is a variant of **Rug Pull** much more difficult to identify. They are **slow** Rug Pulls.

They are also less dangerous as their purpose is not to generate a single price **Dump**.

The **Tokens** are distributed evenly among all participants of the Rug Pull.

When the token starts collecting investments and therefore rising in price, the **rugpullers** will start selling all their **Cryptos** little by little, until emptying the wallet.

The project will not collapse from one moment to the next but the investors' money will end up on a **useless token**, abandoned by the developers.

## How can I avoid them?

Understanding if a project can be a **Rug Pull** is necessary if we want to buy on **Decentralized Exchanges** (which I advise against if you are not an expert).

The most important clue comes from the **Liquidity Pool**.

It contains all the **Tokens** made available by the creators, so initially all those in circulation.

Users will be able to exchange tokens like **Ethereum** for that specific Crypto. The **Liquidity Pool** will be emptied of proprietary Token and filled with currencies like indeed **ETH**.

The creators of the project can withdraw all this **capital**, emptying the Liquidity Pool.

Happy ending for the **scammers**: they disappear into thin air with millions of dollars while all **investors** find themselves full of tokens without any value.

But the **Liquidity Pool** can be locked.

It is a move that all serious projects are doing by now. They show the **investor** that they can trust the project and that there is no possibility of **Rug Pull**.

A **Smart Contract** blocks for a certain amount of time the withdrawal of tokens from the **Pool**.
The absence of this block is an important signal of possible **scam**.

Another check you will have to do, both on **Decentralized Exchanges** and Centralized ones, is on the creators of the project.

Visit the website, check who the **creators** are (if there aren't any abandon the idea of buying the token except in rare cases) and read the **Whitepaper**.

This information, read with a critical eye, can give you an idea about the **reliability** of the project.

If you have even just a minimal doubt, do not buy.

Be very careful also about the opinions you read online: try to enter the right **communities** and do not trust what you read. Remember that most people know as much as you if not even less.

In general, a good piece of advice I can give you is not to buy **tokens** just released on the market.

Often **investors** are attracted by these projects because being poorly **capitalized** they have a greater **growth margin**.
It is never like that, you will only end up hurting yourself.

### Avoid slow Rug Pulls

The further check to avoid also slow **Rug Pulls** must be done using **Etherscan**.

A tool for analyzing blocks and statistics of the **Ethereum** network. Thanks to it we could check all the amounts deposited in the various **wallets**.

You will have to check the quantity of **tokens** to ensure that there are no large **wallets** with the exact same amount of currency.
