A **Search Engine** is a **Web Technology** used to search the **Internet** for the information you request, returning it in the form of an index or ranking of the best results found.

Through an **algorithm**, it analyzes which information is most relevant to your search, and therefore which could be the best based on many factors such as: **user experience** (time to first byte, interaction speed, smoothness, etc...) and **content quality**.

Over time, content creators on the Web realized there was a way to "**hack**" the algorithm, that is, to manage to be the page most appreciated by the engine, through a series of accuracies (SEO, we'll talk about it later).

When talking about **search engines**, one always thinks of only one, which has a complete monopoly on [Web](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0/) search: **Google**.

Google, combining the use of its text search with image search and YouTube (which is owned by Google), **exceeds 90% of global searches**.

## How a search engine works

### Part One

To "capture" the information present on the Internet, the search engine uses software that roams the Web looking for new pages, called "**Crawlers**".

This serves to gather everything that is uploaded online, so as to then have the information from which to choose the best ones to rank.

### Part Two

In the second part, Google must decide which of those pages will be **indexed**.

It may be that the search engine itself chooses not to index them for some particular reason or that the post author gives the indication to Google.

### Part Three

In the third and final part, the search engine must **respond to the user** who uses it to search.

Anyone searching for anything on Google does so through a **query**: a set of keywords useful to identify exactly what we want to know; for this reason, articles and prepositions are often not used.

In response to our query, the Search Engine ranks by **relevance**, according to the algorithm it uses, the best pages among those indexed.

Obviously, its purpose is to give us the **best existing information**, and this is exactly what [**Google's Artificial Intelligence**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/intelligenza-artificiale-nella-vita-quotidiana/) is for.

## The algorithm: how Google decides who to reward

The **algorithm** used by Google to understand what might be the best information for the user is updated very frequently.

These updates are done mainly for two reasons:

* Improve the **intelligence of the algorithm**
* Do not allow content creators to understand it completely

Both reasons are driven by Google's desire not to allow poor-quality content to be among the **top searches**, which could happen if the algorithm were not updated frequently.

By now, some choices that our search engine likes are public knowledge, but they alone will never be enough to bring a page to the top of the ranking.

This is because Google uses **hundreds of different parameters**, some not "hackable", and also because the artificial intelligence of the algorithm is increasing more and more.

Google's [**AI**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) Intelligence is now capable of recognizing the meaning of a sentence, and you can imagine how much this improves the quality of its choices.

In addition, many other data are analyzed such as the **number of links** from other sites pointing to yours, or for example the **average dwell time** on a page.

So if your article is of poor value and readers don't like it, abandoning it in 20 seconds, Google **penalizes** you by lowering you in searches.

The search engine is also able to understand our "**expertise**" on a specific topic.

We all know [**Aranzulla**](https://www.aranzulla.it), and as you well know when talking about technology it is difficult not to find him among the **first results**.

However, if he decided to post on his Blog an article where he talks about the best face creams, despite being Aranzulla, he would not be rewarded with a high position in the [**SERP**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine_results_page).

This is precisely because Google has managed to implement a technology capable of recognizing this as well.

All the information listed above may seem confusing, but it is also of great help for those interested in creating a website.

However, know that the most important metric is always the same: the **quality of information**.

## Positioning techniques: SEO

**SEO** is the acronym for Search Engine Optimization, which is the set of operations performed on a website to facilitate its indexing and improve its **ranking**.

As I mentioned in the previous paragraph, these precautions are important, but they remain secondary to the main goal which is to give users a **valid information product**.

## What is not shown in search results

If you believe that the Web is a safe place where **illegal activities** cannot take place, you are wrong.

The truth is that Google is a safe place because it filters pages to index and those not to index, removing illegal topics, but not the entire Internet.

In fact, there is a place called "**Deep Web**" (or Dark Web) where pages are found that voluntarily did not want to position themselves on search engines because they deal with **forbidden topics**.

Obviously, there are still alternative methods to find these pages, otherwise, they would have no reason to exist, but this does not happen with the search engines we use daily.

## The power of search engines

We have just talked about how Google or more generally search engines can decide whether to put a specific page **at the top of searches** for a query, but what we haven't talked about is the power Google has over us.

This discussion is valid only for those who own an indexed website, not for a normal user.

Google as a search engine, just as it can **bring us visits**, can also take them away.

In fact, it is free to **eliminate our entire website** overnight, without giving any explanation, and this is the defect of a still **centralized** Web.

If we are attacked by [**Negative-SEO**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/negative-seo/) through junk [**Backlinks**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backlink) and we don't remedy it immediately, we could see our site lose hundreds of positions in the search engine ranking.

In short, Google can do whatever it wants with our site, and it is not rare that an algorithm change can penalize us enormously, without any change to our pages.

As you will have already understood, managing a website can be very complicated and requires a lot of attention and consistency because it is necessary to be always updated on **Google's needs**. It is the one in charge.
