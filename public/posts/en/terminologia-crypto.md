## Crypto Terminology: Fundamental Terms

Let's start with the most important **Crypto Terminology**, which you have probably already heard of.

I want to clarify that mine are not dictionary definitions, but explanations written by me with the aim of making you understand what we are talking about as best as possible.

### Bitcoin

[**Bitcoin**](https://bitcoin.org/it/) is the first successful Cryptocurrency project. It is a **virtual currency**: it does not exist in a physical version but only **digital**. It is scarce, there are only 21 million of them, and for this reason, if many people want to buy it, its value increases.



It can be used both as a **currency of exchange** instead of Euro or Dollar, and as a **store of value** instead of Gold. For both uses there are problems, the main one is its instability: since there are still relatively few owners, its value fluctuates much faster than both gold and forex currencies.

The true value of **Bitcoin** is the technology with which it was created: the **Blockchain**.

The vocabulary on **crypto terminology** could not fail to start here.

### Cryptocurrencies

**Cryptocurrencies** are digital currencies based on **Blockchain** technology.

What differentiates them from using a credit card are mainly two things: the fact that there is no physical counterpart and the Blockchain technology.

Your money on the credit card or bank account is a figure representing how many banknotes you possess.

The number of Euros in circulation will always be the number of printed banknotes, while **Bitcoins** exist only in the digital world.

The second difference, however, is the most important of all. If the only difference were the one just listed, Bitcoin and other **Cryptocurrencies** would not be so special.

They would simply be other currencies, equal to Euro and Dollar, but not present in physical format. Equivalent therefore to a hypothetical Digital Euro.

Their utility would be zero: even currencies used today can circulate without the exchange of physical objects (banknotes, coins).

What makes them special is the **Blockchain**.

### Blockchain

The **Blockchain** is probably the most difficult concept to understand in **crypto terminology**. It is a real computer technology that can have many other applications besides **Cryptocurrencies**.



To list a few: [**NFT**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/blockchain/nft/), [**Metaverse**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/blockchain/metaverso/), **Smart Contract** and [**Internet of Things**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/linternet-delle-cose/).

The great capability of the Blockchain is to provide completely **transparent** and **secure** information quickly, this means that movements and transactions are visible to everyone.

Blockchains are facing a complicated problem that does not allow expressing their potential. This problem is called the Blockchain Trilemma and all the largest Blockchains are studying possible resolutions.

At the same time, however, no one can modify them. This means that if an error occurs, it will be fixed through another **transaction**, always public.

This **transparency** and this efficiency allow the Blockchain to be the future technology for contract management and data exchange.

As implied by the name "**Crypto-currencies**", the Blockchain leaves users anonymous, encrypting information. Everyone will be able to see the transaction ledger but it will still maintain user **privacy**, indicating the wallet code and not personal data.

No one will be able to know who owns a specific wallet.

Simply put, the Blockchain is a **chain of information** divided into blocks, which remain unchanged over time.

This chain has a **decentralized** operation and will always be visible over time.

It is the best way to store data when priority is placed on information **security**.

### Proof of Stake and Proof of Work

They are two **algorithms** that allow the functioning of **cryptocurrencies**. They are two consensus algorithms that serve to validate network **transactions**.



**Proof of Work (PoW)** is the method used by Bitcoin, while **Proof of Stake (PoS)** is used by Cryptocurrencies like Ethereum, Polkadot, Binance Coin (BNB) and Solana.

Proof of Work was the first algorithm implemented but today Proof of Stake is preferred. Mainly old cryptos still use **PoW**, while all new projects have moved or were born with **PoS**.

The reason why it is preferred is its **energy expenditure**, much lower than the first. Besides this also the fact that, due to the high cost of supercomputers required for **mining**, PoW is becoming increasingly **centralized**.

But how do they work?

Proof of Work consists of a Mining process, a process in which many computers work with the aim of verifying **network transactions**, solving complex problems. This work done by computers is very tiring and for this reason mining has a large expenditure of **energy**.

Proof of Stake instead is much simpler. There is no problem to solve, the algorithm works through the "**Staking**" of the cryptocurrency, which consists only in locking a certain number for a determined or undetermined time.

Both Algorithms reward the user who provides computing power or who locks their **cryptocurrencies**, minting new ones. This is how new Bitcoins or new Ethereums are placed on the market.

### Mining

**Mining** is the process of computing **diffusion** typical of Bitcoin and Proof of Work cryptocurrencies. Mining is the process that leads to the addition of new **blocks** to the Blockchain chain, through the validation of transactions.

Mining obtains computing power from computers all over the world, anyone can mine with their **computing** devices (usually large computers) and for this reason the mining process is **decentralized**.

Simply put, people provide the power of their computer to validate **bitcoin** transactions and receive other bitcoins in exchange.

**New cryptocurrencies** are abandoning mining in favor of staking due to the large energy expenditure of the former.

### Staking

**Staking** is the process "opposed" to mining. Not in the sense that they are opposite in operation but because they are the two most widespread types for the functioning of a cryptocurrency **protocol**.

Mining is now giving way to staking which for some years has been preferred for its lower energy expenditure.

Unlike mining, it does not require solving **complex problems** for currency distribution and transaction validation.

The only thing staking requires, and what it consists of, is to keep a certain amount of the cryptocurrency in question **locked**.

Every person who does so will receive a reward based on the amount of the figure in **staking** and the period of time for which it will be done.

## Crypto Terminology: Secondary Terms

After seeing all the fundamental terms, without which all the next information makes no sense, let's move on to **Secondary Crypto Terminology**.

They are very used in the Cryptocurrency world and probably if you clicked on this page it is because you are looking for the **meaning** of one of the following.

Some of the terms will not be exclusive to cryptocurrencies but also used in other **financial fields**.

### Hodl

The term **Hodl** is a misspelling of the word Hold which in the financial field means keeping one's assets in the portfolio without selling them. This misspelling was born many years ago on the Web in a Bitcoin **forum** due to a typing error.

From that moment on the word HODL spread more and more until being used still today as an alternative to the word Hold. Usable for any Investment Asset, it has a meaning similar to that of the original word but in an even stronger sense.

It was then made the acronym for "**Hold On for Dear Life**", which gives a good idea of its meaning.

In fact, the term is used especially in moments of great market crash, precisely indicating that even in the worst moments they will not sell the asset.

Used especially by strong supporters of **Cryptocurrencies** who intend to **never sell** their Cryptocurrencies.

It is however considered a playful term that is not used in professional speeches but more in informal exchanges on the web.

### Crypto Market Cap

The [**Crypto Market Cap**](https://it.tradingview.com/markets/cryptocurrencies/global-charts/) is the **capitalization** of the entire Cryptocurrency market. It is an indicator not used for trading or technical analysis, but more created to analyze what market situation one is in.

Useful for checking the general state of the cryptocurrency **market**.

### Crypto Wallet

A [**Wallet**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/dove-conservare-criptovalute/) is as if it were your **wallet** but with Cryptocurrencies, instead of being physical it is a digital place which you access with password, pin or **seeds**.



There are different types: cold, hot and hard.

### Cold Wallet, Hot Wallet and Hard wallet

Although one might think there aren't many differences between wallet types, it's not so.

**Hot wallets** are online wallets, like those present in every **Exchange**. When you buy cryptocurrencies on an Exchange platform, they are transferred to your **wallet**.

That wallet is your hot wallet on the Exchange. It is very convenient because from there you can sell or buy in very little time.

Being an online Wallet however, holding **cryptocurrencies** there for prolonged periods of time, exposes you to the risk of Hacker attacks. A Cold Wallet is much safer.

A **Cold wallet** is an offline wallet. It is much more inconvenient than a hot wallet because you cannot buy cryptocurrencies directly from there. The procedure will therefore be: buy them on the exchange and send them from the exchange's hot wallet to the cold wallet.

It is a decidedly longer procedure but allows you to have your cryptocurrencies in a much safer place.

The cold wallet, being **offline**, can be accessed only via its "password", called "**seed**". Without it, no one will ever manage to access it.

The **Hard Wallet** is a cold wallet with the difference that it will not be on your smartphone but in a separate physical device like a **USB drive**.

### DApps

[**DApps**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0-dapps/) stands for **Decentralized Applications**. When talking about DApps we mean all applications that work on Blockchain and therefore are **free** and not controlled by a centralized entity.

All app control is in the hands of the **users**.

Examples of Centralized applications are Instagram, Facebook and Whatsapp. They are controlled by the company **Meta**, which decides the rules and can also block access to its services if it wants.

Decentralized applications serve to avoid this, some decentralized applications are: [**Metamask**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/metamask/) or some **minor Exchanges**.

### Fear and Greed Index

The [**Fear and Greed Index**](https://alternative.me/crypto/fear-and-greed-index/) is an index widely used to analyze people's sentiment regarding markets. It is not an index only concerning **Cryptocurrencies** but is widely used also in this field.

It goes from 0 to 100. In case of extreme fear the indicator lowers, while in case of great confidence it rises.

Usually if the indicator is close to the minimums it is a good time to **buy** and vice versa.

### Fomo

**Fear Of Missing Out**, is the typical feeling that arises when one feels the impulse to invest in an asset that has just undergone a rise, for the fear of being cut out of a **gain**.

This haste to invest, based only on the rise of a stock or **cryptocurrency**, can be very dangerous, especially because it goes against market laws which state: "sell when others buy, buy when others sell".

### Bearish and Bullish

**Bearish** and **Bullish** are two typical words in **investment** slang.

The terms symbolize two animals symbol of finance, the bear (**bear**) and the bull (**bull**).



As the bear symbolizes falling prices, the term Bearish referred to a chart means that a drop is predicted.

Same thing but opposite for the term Bullish which is used to indicate an **upward** view.

### Pump and Dump

The **Pump** and **Dump** is a scam that consists in exponentially increasing the price of a poorly capitalized **asset**, with the aim of selling then at the peak, making all the people involved lose a lot of money.

Let me explain better. It is usually used by groups of people or people with large capital because a lot of money is needed to move the **price** by high percentages. It is frequently used on small cryptocurrencies due to the ease with which the price can be moved when they are still little known.

When the price rises a lot inexperienced people are attracted who fall into the trap and invest in the **crypto** in question. Precisely at that moment the perpetrators of the theft sell their possessions making the **price collapse**.

The only ones to earn are therefore them, who had purchased the **asset** at a low price.

### Fud

**FUD** stands for "**Fear, Uncertainty and Disinformation**". So there is FUD on the market when an air of fear and uncertainty is perceived, often caused also by news not so important or even unfounded.

In general FUD is unfounded and hasty fear, which often leads to selling at the **wrong moment**, as soon as the price drops slightly or as soon as frightening news comes out.

### To the moon

**To the moon** translated into **financial language** means predicting an exponential increase in prices.

It is not as technical a term as "**Bullish**" but is used especially for cryptocurrencies, more to support than to give a technical opinion.

If I believe in Bitcoin I can say "to the moon", but if after a technical analysis I believe prices can rise I say I have a "Bullish" view.

### Rug pull

By the term **Rug Pull** is meant a scam by the creators of a **Crypto project**.

A Rug Pull happens when the intent of the creators is to create a project with the sole purpose of abandoning it and running away with the **investors'** money.

To avoid falling into a Rug Pull the best thing to do is to **analyze** a project carefully before investing in it. Check the **Whitepaper** and who the creators are.

### Private key

The Private Key is simply your **password** to access your cryptocurrencies, whether you keep them in an **online** or **offline portfolio**.

### Smart Contract

[**Smart Contracts**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web-3-0/), are computer protocols similar to normal contracts but that work automatically.



They are much more efficient than normal contracts because thanks to the **Blockchain** they can be created with an "If this - than that" formula, i.e., they can be automated.

The Blockchain allows us to have secure **digital information**, so the smart contract, which needs legal work only in drafting, can then work automatically.

This means that as soon as something described in the contract happens, automatically, it updates and performs its role **autonomously**.

Many things can be done with this technology, from event tickets to salaries, all without human intermediaries.

If Blockchain technology were used for plane tickets, you would be reimbursed instantly in case of flight cancellation.

The best known Cryptocurrencies that allow Smart Contracts at the moment are: **Ethereum**, **Binance**, **Cardano** and **Ripple**.

### Coin Burning

**Coin Burning** is a process used by Cryptocurrency projects to reduce supply.

The supply is reduced precisely by "**burning**" a quantity of Crypto. Usually this procedure helps the **Cryptocurrency** maintain its value higher.

As you well know the value of an **asset** is regulated by the relationship between supply and demand, so reducing the latter it can rise more easily.

### ICO and IEO

The ICO is the **Initial Coin Offering** and is the same thing as an IPO but for cryptocurrencies instead of stocks.

It is the launch offer of a project, which collects initial **investments** for market entry.

Obviously buying through an **ICO**, if the project is valid, pays off. Clearly it is difficult and risky to identify valid projects when they have yet to land on the **market**.

It is easy to find valid projects but that will never start, or find projects that seemed valid but turn out not to be.

Without external opinions understanding the validity of a project is very complicated. At the same time the ICO is fundamental to help and support **startups**.

[List of upcoming ICOs](https://coinmarketcap.com/it/ico-calendar/upcoming/).

**IEOs** are a system born after ICOs that is becoming very popular.

The only difference is that **tokens** are launched on an intermediary platform like an **Exchange** instead of directly through the project as in the previous case.

This method maintains the advantages of ICOs but eliminates many of its defects. In fact if a cryptocurrency is launched on an **exchange** a large audience already starts: the entire user base of the platform.

Furthermore if the project is launched for example on **Binance**, we can rest easier regarding scam risk, as the Exchange itself will have already verified its validity.

It therefore decreases the risk for **initial investors**.

The legitimacy and reputation of the Exchange also makes users who buy through it safer.

### Airdrop

An **Airdrop** is a free distribution of **Cryptocurrencies**. Most of the time actions are required by users who want to access it, to not allow everyone to get them **for free**.

Often **staking** of a major cryptocurrency is required. Other times instead they are sent randomly to some users so you have to be lucky.

Obviously don't think of receiving millions of euros, probably they are very low figures, which however can increase a lot in case the airdropped project goes well.

In these cases in fact it is convenient for you to keep the obtained cryptocurrency instead of selling it for a negligible profit.

### Peer to peer

A peer to peer connection (**p2p**) is a connection from an individual to another, in a **network** where everyone is on the same level.

It is literally an exchange between peers. The **Blockchain** is based on **peer to peer** interactions and exchanges: on the network there are no central servers but only nodes of equal value.

### Atomic Swap

An **atomic swap** is a cryptocurrency transfer directly on Blockchain without any Exchange as **intermediary**.

It is the real reason why the blockchain itself was created, eliminate intermediaries and give **power to individuals**.

### Dao

A **DAO** is a **Decentralized Autonomous Organization** and is an organization controlled entirely by algorithms, by **Smart Contracts**. Once Smart contracts are generated it works totally **automatically**.

Basically it is a system that serves to make everything more reliable, secure and transparent.
It is however a broad and complex topic on which I will write a separate article so it makes no sense to dwell further.

### Fork

A **Fork** of a cryptocurrency happens when the **community** decides to make changes to its **protocol**.

However, it happens only for major updates, those of the original code, otherwise it takes another name of minor importance.

Usually the purpose is to improve the speed of **transactions** but countless things can be done, such as completely separating the old **code** from the new one, generating two distinct Blockchains.

### Whale

**Whales** are those investors who own a huge slice of a certain **asset**, sometimes so large as to control the market.

Monitoring their **movements** is a very frequent activity, as they can be important **signals** for the market.
