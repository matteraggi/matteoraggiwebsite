## What history teaches us

Over time, man has changed the system of **exchange** between individuals several times. Thousands of years ago **barter** was used, exchanging goods for goods.

With the **evolution of the species**, human beings realized that it was more convenient to use rare metals to buy and sell objects, and thus coins were born.

After coins came **paper money**, which for the first time did not have an **intrinsic value** due to the material it was made of, but only a value symbolized by the number written on it.

They were correlated to the **gold reserves** that nations possessed (Gold Standard), so it was not possible to print **infinite** copies, still having a rarity linked to the precious metal.

The main problems were caused by the abandonment of the Gold Standard and therefore with the green light for Nations to produce as many banknotes as they wanted.

Here comes **inflation**. The purchasing power of a currency is also given by its **rarity**, so if this is lost, the currency **devalues**.

The last step was that of **credit cards**, therefore the digitization of money.
