## What is a DCA (PAC)

**PAC** stands for **Capital Accumulation Plan** (in English, usually referred to as **DCA** - Dollar Cost Averaging).

It is an **investment mode** that involves a periodic purchase of an **asset** over time.

Basically, instead of investing **€1000** all at once, I decide to put €150 on the first of every month.

The **DCA** is a long-term investment method, very long.

In fact, it only makes sense if we intend to leave our **savings** untouched for years. It is a technique that will bring us benefits in the future.

Yes, I know, it doesn't seem very exciting, in fact, it isn't.

It is certainly more fun to invest in some high-risk but high-**yield** **cryptocurrency**, hoping to get a **100%** return within a few weeks.

Here, however, we are not talking about **trading** and not even gambling.

The Capital Accumulation Plan is the best way to build our capital. Whether it is a **Crypto DCA** or on a stock fund.

### What is Dollar Cost Averaging

To better understand the **Accumulation Plan**, we must talk about [**Dollar Cost Averaging**](https://alittlemore.net/che-cose-il-dollar-cost-average-pac/).

It consists of spreading out the purchase of a **stock** or any other **investment asset**.

The purchase is therefore made periodically, with the aim of keeping an average **purchase price** quite low.

By buying on both **highs** and **lows**, you will have an excellent average price.

This term was coined by **Benjamin Graham** in his famous book "**The Intelligent Investor**".

## DCA vs Lump Sum (PAC vs PIC)

The [**PIC**](https://investimi.com/pic/) (Piano di Investimento di Capitale) stands for **Lump Sum Investing**.

This complicated name just indicates the classic investment method: the **single purchase**.

We have €500 and we buy €500 of **Bitcoin**, here is a Lump Sum investment.

The problem with the **Lump Sum** is one: buying at the right time.

You can choose a valid **asset**, but if you buy it at the wrong time it will be useless, you will probably lose money anyway.

This is because **timing** is the most relevant factor. With a **DCA** this problem does not arise.

You will have no doubt about when it would have been better to **invest**.
**You will not risk** choosing the wrong moment.

I know what you are thinking... "but if I **invest everything immediately** and then it goes up, I **earn more**"

And you are right.

But the **Accumulation Plan** serves to mitigate **market fluctuations** and to invest in the long term. What does it mean?

Obviously, in case the **asset** gains value immediately, the first option would be winning, but **guessing** the precise moment when the asset will rise is impossible.

It is more likely that it goes down a bit, then goes up again, then goes down again, and so on.

In this case, an **Accumulation Plan** is better, which **protects** us more if the price collapses.

If you are sure that what you bought will gain **value** over time, then you have to think differently.

It does not matter what the **price movement** will be just after the purchase, but the value it will have in the years to come is much more important.

## Pros and Cons of DCA

### Pros

#### Start with little money

If you don't have much money to put aside but have a job that brings you **stable income** every month, a **DCA** is perfect.

Put a part, even small, of your salary into an **Accumulation Plan**.

If you don't have much money and not even a salary, don't think about investing, that comes later.

Even if you have already saved a lot of money, the **DCA** is the best option, especially for the following point.

#### Reduce risk

Averaging the **purchase price** reduces investment risk.

It allows you to **lose** less money in market crashes and to have an average purchase price that will be fine in every situation.

Remember that you will never know what the market **bottom** is. And not even the **top**.

#### Less stress

This can also be a consequence of the previous point.

You will certainly have **less stress**.
Because the risk is lower.

And because you won't have **invested** all your money overnight.

You will be more aware and the **invested funds** will grow gradually over time.

Furthermore, you will learn to think **long term**. This is very important also because it makes you much less anxious.

We must not live as a function of our **investments**. One of the strengths of the **Accumulation Plan** is precisely that of having to spend little time on it.

We can work and live our lives while the **fund** grows by itself.

#### No chart study

The **DCA** allows you to invest without being an expert in **chart analysis**.

In fact, you can even stop looking at them. This is another great advantage.

Chart analysis is useless in an **Accumulation Plan**. In any case, we will invest our money on the set day.

Those who have tried **trading** know how many hours are needed on charts to understand minimally how the market moves.

[**Profitable traders**](https://antoniocarnevaletrading.com/5-regole-per-iniziare-a-fare-trading-ed-essere-profittevoli/) are those who have spent years doing just that.

We cannot pretend to be good at analyzing a chart if we dedicate 10 minutes a day to it.

Luckily with an **Accumulation Plan**, we won't need this skill.

### Cons

Hard to find cons to the **Accumulation Plan**. It can be argued that this technique, if the price rises, is less efficient.

But as we have already discussed before, it is also more **efficient** if the market crashes, making you lose **less money**.

So, considering that we can never be 100% sure that the price will rise, this is a poor criticism.

It is certainly not a technique recommended for impatient people.

You have to be aware that the **results** will be seen only after years.

The last negative point is the **high costs** it can entail.

In fact, having to **invest** many times (monthly but also weekly in some cases), if the **commissions** are high we would end up paying very high figures just to the platform.

For this reason, it is very important to choose a **platform** with low fees. Usually, [**Exchanges**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/quale-exchange/) do not have very high ones.

### Crypto DCA: Initial Amount And Installments

The **initial amount** depends on how much money you have available, as do the installments.

You can choose to pay a larger first **installment**, therefore starting with a higher initial **amount**, or to start with a normal installment.

For the frequency of installments, it depends a lot on you. Usually, I recommend a **monthly cadence**.

It is frequent enough but does not require many entries (and therefore more commissions) like a weekly **DCA** (which however would mediate the price more).

## Crypto DCA Tips

Managing a **DCA** can be complicated. I know it seems like a simple thing, but continuing it for years trust me it is not.

When you find yourself deciding whether to put money into the DCA or spend it on a better vacation, you will be very, very **undecided**.

For this reason, **automatic payment** is very useful, even if it can be easily cancelled. It is not a definitive solution.

The only solution is to have **discipline** and always remember why you are doing it.

Another piece of advice I can give you is to put your cryptocurrencies on a [**Hard Wallet**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/dove-conservare-criptovalute/).

Having to **hold** them for years it is recommended to keep them in the most **secure** place possible.

A Hard wallet is a **physical offline wallet**, impossible to hack.

Instead, exchange wallets (**Hot Wallets**) are subject to **Hacker attacks**.

The most famous and used hard wallet is the [**Ledger**](https://www.ledger.com).

A ledger, however, can be a not insignificant **expense**. A free middle ground is [**Metamask**](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/metamask/), a **cold wallet** (offline) but not physical. It is still much safer than keeping them on an **Exchange**.
