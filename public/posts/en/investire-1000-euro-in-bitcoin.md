How much can one earn?

- it is a more **volatile** asset than stocks

What can we draw from these statements?

Bitcoin is a **risky asset**. It can promise huge gains but also huge losses.

Furthermore, its value, as a medium of exchange, is based on supply and demand. And demand is an unpredictable variable.

Despite this, its characteristics as a scarce resource could (assuming constant demand) lead the price to have a logarithmic **trend** graph.

So a percentage drop in profits, but also a decrease in **volatility**. This, however, is a future hypothesis.

## Decide the 4 pillars

What are these **4 pillars**?

- time horizon
- risk tolerance
- investment mode
- financial goal

Let's start with the first: you will have to decide for how long you intend to keep your **investments**.

There is no need for a precise date, but rather a time frame.

For example: within this year, a few years, decades.

The more extended our **time frame** is, the less we will care about daily price fluctuations.

Second point: your risk tolerance.

This is actually not a decision to make, because we cannot decide it.

It is more about understanding how much you would be able to detach yourself from **market fluctuations**.

And be careful. Many overestimate their abilities and it can be a very costly mistake.

This choice will also influence the next point.

Third point: investment mode.

Based on your time horizon and your risk tolerance, you will choose the **investment mode**.

What modes exist?

- Lump Sum (single investment)
- [DCA](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/pac-criptovalute/) (Dollar Cost Averaging / Accumulation Plan)

Single investment is recommended for those with good risk tolerance and a narrow time horizon.

The **Accumulation Plan (DCA)** is my favorite technique and it is the one I will recommend to you.

It involves investing a fixed amount periodically (weekly or monthly).

## Protect your Bitcoin

Once purchased, **Bitcoins** cannot be held in an Exchange.

It is dangerous, they can be stolen with a **Hacker** attack. There are safer places instead.

These are **Cold Wallets**: offline wallets. Disconnected from the network, therefore impossible to reach from the outside.

The procedure is very simple. Just send the **Cryptocurrencies** to the address of the offline wallet.

It can be of several types: digital or physical. But the substance does not change.

In fact, in any case, the procedure is the same.

You can do it monthly or even less frequently. The important thing is to remember every now and then so as not to leave most Bitcoins on an online account.
