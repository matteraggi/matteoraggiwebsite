Is it the same as the classic wallet?

I'm talking about the types of Wallets that exist. Some are more similar to the classic **wallet**, as I just told you, others however we could compare to a **safe**.

But let's see one thing at a time. What are these wallets?

- Hot Wallet
- [Cold Wallet](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/cold-wallet/)
- Hard Wallet

Hot Wallets are the first on the list but the last I will explain to you, then you will understand why.

Cold Wallets instead, comparable to the operation of a safe, are **offline wallets**.

What does offline wallet mean?

It means they are wallets not connected to the network. From them, you can neither receive nor send **crypto**.

You may be wondering what they are for... think of the comparison with the safe.

They serve solely to **keep** them. Because not being connected to the network they are super secure and are not subject to hacker attacks.

**Hard Wallets** are a subset of the Cold Wallets we just talked about.

They have the exact same purpose and behavior, except for one difference.

The fact that they are physical.

*But how, in 2023, when everything is moving online and especially in the world of digital payments, crypto wallets are physical?*

Yes, because they are the most **secure**. Cold Wallets are also very secure, but physical wallets more so.

They are small, the most famous has the shape of a USB stick: it is called [Ledger](https://shop.ledger.com/?r=b3cfdb42defc).

Now that you understand how to keep **Cryptocurrencies** without the fear of losing them due to a Hacker attack, let's talk about Hot Wallets.

Why did I leave them for last? Because **Metamask** is precisely an Hot Wallet.

The difference between Cold Wallets, as is intuitive, is given by the fact that they are connected to the network, they are **online**.

This feature allows wallets to manage to communicate with external services and send and receive **Crypto**.

The defect corresponds instead with the strong point of their "brothers": security.

The wallet – safe comparison holds once again.

Money in the safe is safe but cannot be spent unless withdrawn from there.

While money in a wallet is easily spendable but not safe.

Just as we don't keep all our money in a wallet, equally in an **Hot Wallet**.

## What is Metamask

Let's start with what we already know: **Metamask** is an Hot Wallet.

However, I'm sorry to inform you, there are also different types of **Hot Wallets**.

But don't worry, there are only two:

- **Custodial** Hot Wallet
- **Non-custodial** Hot Wallet

Custodial Hot Wallets are those that keep the **private keys** for you.

What is a private key?

Basically, it is what you need to access the wallet, and it should not be shared with anyone.

Then there is also the **public key**. It serves to identify you on the network and therefore also to receive payments.

The types of Hot Wallets that keep the private key for you are usually those of [Exchanges](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/migliori-exchange-crypto/).

*"Keeping Crypto in an Exchange Wallet is like not really owning them"*

It is a phrase I have heard thousands of times. It has, however, a grain of truth.

Just as the money you have in the bank is managed by them, the **Crypto** you have on a custodial wallet is managed by third parties.

And this goes against one of the values that led to the creation of Cryptocurrencies: the true possession of money by the individual.

For this reason, I advise against holding them on **wallets** of that type (obviously in small quantities it is fine).

Finally, here we are at **Non-custodial Wallets**, where the individual owns their private keys.

Metamask is one of those.

It is based on the **Ethereum network** and is among the most used platforms.

It can be used as a browser extension (not on safari) or as a mobile application.

What it does is create a link from Web 2.0 (what we use today) to [Web 3.0](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/web3/). The third version of the Web consists of all DApps (decentralized applications).

Creating the wallet is very simple and guided.

But once we put our Crypto on **Metamask**, what can we do?

## How to create a wallet

Metamask can be used both as a **browser** extension and from a **mobile** device.

Usually, since most decentralized applications are usable only from PC, it is used as an extension.

Let's see how to configure the **wallet** in both cases.

### Metamask on Chrome (extension)

First install the extension from the official site.

Once installed proceed to configuration. Since it will be your first access you will have to follow this path:

get started **>** create a wallet **>** I agree

Then you will have to create a **Password** that you will need every time to access, make sure it is difficult to **crack** as it will be your wallet password.

A backup **seed phrase** (the wallet key) will be created which will serve precisely in case you don't remember the password.

The seed phrase corresponds to the **private key**. In fact, through a particular function (hash) the first is transformed into the second, but not vice versa.

It will be composed of **12 English words** not connected by any logical sense.

It is important to keep this phrase in a **safe place** and never lose it. Do not write it on your phone or computer, but on a piece of paper.

In the next page you will be asked to rewrite it for security.

And there you go! The **wallet** has been created.

## FAQ

**How much does MetaMask cost?**

Metamask is a free Hot Wallet. It earns by taking fees that always remain below 1%.

**What crypto does MetaMask accept?**

Metamask being a Wallet and not an Exchange, does not "accept" Cryptocurrencies but "Supports". Metamask supports all Ethereum network Cryptocurrencies (being a Wallet based on that network), so all ERC20 tokens.

**How to receive money on MetaMask?**

Like any movement of Cryptocurrencies it happens with a Crypto transfer from a Wallet address to your Metamask Wallet address.
