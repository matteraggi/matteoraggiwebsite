## What are Cryptocurrencies

**Investing in Cryptocurrencies** is different from investing in the stock market.
This is precisely because Cryptocurrencies are different from the shares of a listed company.

When you invest in Cryptocurrencies you are not actually buying anything.

What does this mean?

Let's take it step by step: let's start with the meaning of their name.

The word Cryptocurrency is composed of two parts: **Crypto** and **Currency**.

Crypto stands for Cryptography.

**Cryptography** is the art of hidden writing. It encompasses all methods to make a message incomprehensible to some, but understandable to others.

In recent years Cryptography has moved predominantly into the digital world. It is therefore about **Cybersecurity**: methods to protect our data from possible Hacker attacks.

And what does this have to do with Cryptocurrencies?

Well, think about it for a moment: without a digital security mechanism we couldn't trust them.

Would you be willing to keep all your money in an unsafe place? Where a malicious person could take it away from you overnight?

It would be like keeping all your money in front of your doorstep. Anyone who wanted to could take it.

**Cryptocurrencies** can function only thanks to their security, inherent in their technology.

The technology behind Cryptocurrencies is called [Blockchain](https://www.matteoraggiblog.com/blockchain-investire/). It is thanks to it that data is Encrypted and protected.

Now let's move on to the second part of the term, decidedly less complex.

"Currencies". They are indeed currencies: like the Euro or the Dollar, but digital.

This brings us back to what I told you at the beginning of the paragraph.

"When you invest in Cryptocurrencies you are not actually buying anything".

You cannot buy Euros or Dollars, because they are currencies, and so are **Cryptos**.

What we are doing when we "buy" Cryptocurrencies is exchanging our FIAT currency (national currency) with a digital currency.

And this digital currency can be any Crypto, such as **Bitcoin**.

## The Bitcoin Whitepaper

Introduction: Why was it created?

The international banking system is a trust-based system. And this is because for every operation there are human intermediaries.

This is not a merit, especially when it comes to money. It also increases transaction costs.

How does **Bitcoin** work?

Being based on an encrypted system, human intermediaries are not needed. This implies greater security for the individual user.

### Transactions

Transactions work like a chain of digital signatures from user to user, in fact, it is a **P2P network**.

*Yes ok, but practically how does it work?*

Basically, I send Bitcoins to you and you send Bitcoins to another. And so a **chain of transactions** is formed.

Every time I send you money both of us must verify that the transaction is valid.

But how can the person receiving money be sure that it hasn't already been spent?

With FIAT currencies, a central authority takes care of it, through which transactions must pass.

To avoid placing all the weight on a single authority, Bitcoin exploits a **decentralized system**.

This system needs two things:

- a public ledger
- users who verify the transaction

The ledger will contain all of them in chronological order.

Users will be able to consult this ledger and agree on the validity of transactions.

And if the majority of users validate the movement, then it will be accepted.

At this point, two doubts might arise:

- How do users **verify transactions**?
- Can it happen that the majority of the network is wrong?

The answer to the first question we will see in the next paragraph, which will be of fundamental importance.

The answer to the second is: Yes but no.

Theoretically yes, but practically it is almost impossible.

It could happen only if a Hacker managed to take possession of half the network. Which is not only practically impossible but would not even be convenient for the hacker.

### Proof of Work

So how do users verify transactions? We're getting there.

First, you need to understand how the **Bitcoin network** is structured in the real world.

The network works as a distributed system of computers scattered around the world. All connected to each other, performing the same operations.

The more users participate, the more secure the network will be.

Each of us can be a **node** (or participant) of the network, just have a computer and make it available.

*What does making it available mean?*

It means **verifying transactions**. Remember the question from before?

When you make a computer available to the network you are providing its computing power to verify transactions.

And transactions are verified through **Proof of Work** (PoW). The PoW process is called **Mining**.

What is required of each computer is the resolution of complicated mathematical algorithms. This work is used for the creation of a block of transactions.

Once the algorithm is solved, the block can be validated and the whole network will verify the operations.

The reward for the work done is **new Bitcoins**. Fewer and fewer as time passes to make the asset deflationary.

If you are already thinking of starting to **mine Bitcoin** to earn, know that it is not that simple.

- It is expensive (computers and electricity)
- You don't earn that much (it is more convenient in cold countries or where electricity costs less)
- It requires attention, more than normally owning Bitcoins

### The network

How does the Bitcoin network run?

This section answers this question, so it digs deeper into what I talked about in the previous paragraph.

Basically, it explains in detail how **transaction verification** takes place.

1. They are grouped into blocks (block-chain)
2. Each user works to solve the algorithm
3. Once solved, forwards the solution to everyone

What happens if two correct solutions are sent simultaneously?

Because yes, it can happen.

In that case, the longest chain "wins", which is considered the safest.

### The incentive

Actually... I already spoiled the content of this paragraph for you.

When a block of transactions is verified, the node that found the solution receives Bitcoins as a reward.

This step is fundamental to allow the network to sustain itself. It serves to reward those who used energy to provide value to Bitcoin.

But it is also a **security system**.

If paradoxically a malicious person managed to appropriate more than half the network, it would be convenient for them to mine instead of sabotaging it.

### Privacy

How does **privacy** work on Bitcoin?

In central banks privacy is maintained by obscuring sensitive data, thus hiding information.

Bitcoin, as you will have understood, makes everything public. Even the **transaction ledger**.

Yet, the Bitcoin network allows a high level of anonymity.
The [public ledger](https://www.blockchain.com/explorer/mempool/btc) is structured this way:

As you may have noticed there are no names, only numbers and codes.

These codes are the **public keys** of all users who are using the network.

Using public keys, that is the codes that identify us on the network (used to exchange Bitcoin), no one will be able to link us to a particular movement of money.

## How to protect your Bitcoin

In 2022 **3.8 billion** dollars were stolen according to [Chainalysis data](https://blog.chainalysis.com/reports/2022-biggest-year-ever-for-crypto-hacking/), through Cryptocurrency Exchanges.

If you don't want to meet the same fate you will have to implement a series of precautions, which most people ignore.

Before introducing this topic you must know the difference between **Hot Wallet** and **Cold Wallet**.

Hot Wallets (e.g. all those on Exchanges) are wallets connected to the network. Being connected to the network they allow you to sell and buy Cryptocurrencies immediately.

On the other side of the coin, they can be subject to **Hacker** attacks.

*We talked until now about how secure Crypto is and then they can steal it from me?*

The truth is that this is not the true nature of Cryptocurrencies. If they are erroneously held in these wallets it is for the following reasons:

- underestimation of risk
- convenience and ease of use

But **Crypto** were created to be held in a Cold Wallet. Only in that way could we consider ourselves completely owners of Bitcoin.

A Cold Wallet is disconnected from the network, so it is really unreachable. It is like a safe, without the security key it cannot be opened.

But it is an extremely resistant safe: nothing can open it, not even a nuclear attack.

Knowing the **private key** of the wallet is the only way.

Obviously, being offline, selling is not that fast. You will first have to move the Bitcoins you want to sell to an Online wallet (a Hot Wallet).

Then there is a type of Cold Wallet called Hard Wallet. They are Cold Wallets that instead of being a program on the computer are physical hardware.
